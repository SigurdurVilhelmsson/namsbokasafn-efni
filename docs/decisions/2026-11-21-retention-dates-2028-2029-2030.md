## Question

The editors' consent text (its private draft, item 14) tells editors how long the editing tool keeps what it records about them. Those dates must be enforced by code, and the research date is a single value (`RETENTION_DATE` in `server/lib/researchRetention.js`) that deletes every raw research row once set, so it may only be set by a reviewed commit citing a public record (SD6). What are the dates, what happens on each, and how are they enforced? At stake: a promise to named people, and an irreversible deletion if a date is wrong or early.

## Decision

| Records | Kept until | Then |
|---|---|---|
| Editing records that name a person (every `sessions.db` table that names an editor, except the accounts table) | **31 December 2028** | names and account ids replaced and times cut to the date; pure logs and the rows linking a person to work deleted |
| Records without a name (served text versions; unit openings and their snapshots) | **31 December 2029** | deleted |
| Raw research rows (the research database) | **31 December 2029** | deleted by `research-retention.js`; a de-identified derived dataset is kept |
| Consent, deletion and loss records | **31 December 2030** | deleted |

A date takes effect at 00:00 UTC on the day after. Each date is enforced **automatically on the editorial backup cron**, before the copy is taken, and **editing ends with 2028**: a record dated after a date is not acted on, and the script refuses and raises the health alarm instead. **The committed glossary record shows the deciding account's role, never its name**, because public git history cannot be stripped.

## Reasoning

### Why these dates

They are [USER]'s consent rulings of 2026-11-09, made with the consent text. The order follows what each kind of record is for: names matter only while the editing they describe is going on; unnamed records support the analysis, which runs a year longer; the raw research rows are deleted after the last planned publication; and the proof of each person's consent and of each deletion is kept longest, so the deletions themselves stay demonstrable.

### Why strip names rather than delete editing records

Deleting the records would lose the per-segment editorial history, which the translation memory and the research report draw on; only the files would remain. Stripping keeps the history usable with no name in it. The consequence is honest wording: a stripped record keeps its book, chapter and module, so someone who knows who edited a chapter can still tell whose it was. The consent text says *names removed*, not *anonymous*.

### Why automatic, and why refuse on later records

A by-hand run depends on someone remembering a date two to four years away. The cron runs every cycle anyway, so a date that passes is acted on within one cycle, and a missed or failed run shows on the health check. Editing is planned to end with 2028; if it does not, acting on the whole database would destroy live work and records the consent does not cover, so the script stops and a person decides.

### Why the glossary record shows a role

`book-glossary.json` is committed to the public repository every two hours. A display name committed there outlives every retention date, because git history cannot be rewritten without destroying everyone's clones. A role label tells a reader of the record what they need: that a head editor decided. Checked 2026-11-21: every committed value, and every one of production's 152 glossary change rows, is the c19 loader's label, so no name has yet been committed.

## Consequences

- `server/lib/retentionDates.js` becomes the one owner of the four dates (an enforceable value with its test); `researchRetention.js`'s `RETENTION_DATE` reads 31 December 2029 from it, citing this record as SD6's.
- The consent text's item 14 says names are removed after 2028 and the records kept without them.
- Continued editing after 2028 needs a new decision before the date: new consent wording and new dates, superseding this record. The script's refusal makes that visible rather than silent.
- Existing backup copies are not touched by the live steps; the 12-month cap on editorial backups (G1.20) is what ends them, and must exist before the consent text is listed.
- Reversal: before a date passes, a new record and a one-line change. After it, the stripped names and deleted rows are gone; only backups younger than the cap still hold them.
- The work and its order are tracked in the register (the c46 entry), not here.

## Alternatives considered

1. **Delete every named editing record on the date** — loses the editorial history the translation memory and report use.
2. **Strip everything and delete nothing** — keeps logs (activity, notifications, locks) that have no use once editing ends.
3. **Run the deletions by hand, with a reminder** — depends on memory years ahead; a missed date breaks the promise silently.
4. **Act only on the consented period while editing continues** — needs per-record survival rules and its own consent; not needed while editing ends with 2028.
5. **Keep display names in the glossary record and say so in the consent text** — a published name can never be withdrawn.
