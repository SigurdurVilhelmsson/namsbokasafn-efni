## Question

The project has been carrying five OpenStax books through one pipeline: `efnafraedi-2e` (Chemistry 2e),
`lifraen-efnafraedi` (Organic Chemistry), `edlisfraedi-2e` (College Physics 2e), `liffraedi-2e` (Biology 2e)
and `orverufraedi` (Microbiology). All five are published on namsbokasafn.is.

Circumstances outside this repository changed. The question was whether to continue carrying five books
at reader-facing quality, or to narrow to a subset — and if narrowing, **what happens to the material
already published**, and **what happens to the source of the books that stop being published**.

What was at stake in the second half of that question: three of the five books hold `01-source/` trees
obtained under licences that can no longer be obtained. OpenStax relicensed on 2026-03-19; the CC BY 4.0
set is closed permanently. Biology (obtained 2026-03-11) and Microbiology (2026-03-09) are CC BY 4.0 —
irrevocable for the copies held here, and unobtainable today. A decision that narrowed *scope* by
deleting *material* would destroy that, one-way.

## Decision

**Focus development exclusively on `efnafraedi-2e` and `lifraen-efnafraedi`.** Retire
`edlisfraedi-2e`, `liffraedi-2e` and `orverufraedi` from namsbokasafn.is, so that readers are served
two books rather than five, and halt translation and editing on them.

**This is a pause, not a withdrawal, and it is explicitly reversible** — work on the other titles may
resume. It is also **not a reorganisation**: nothing is touched in chemistry or organic, and the
pipeline does not change.

**Delete nothing, in either repository.** `01-source/` and its licence provenance, existing MT output,
translation memory, glossaries, embed mappings, slug maps, reference translations **and the rendered
`05-publication/` trees** all stay exactly as they are, for all five books. Retirement is a *visibility*
decision, not a *deletion* decision.

**The retirement is implemented in namsbokasafn-vefur — in how it syncs and builds content for the
website.** No efni artifact is removed or moved to achieve it.

The sequencing and status of the withdrawal operation belong to the active register, not to this record.

## Reasoning

### Narrowing the published surface, not the archive, is what the licence situation demands

The three withdrawn books' `01-source/` trees are legally load-bearing and, for two of them, irreplaceable.
Measured for this record: `books/liffraedi-2e/01-source/media/` holds **2,455 files, 0 of them tracked in
git**; `books/edlisfraedi-2e/01-source/media/` holds **2,088, likewise 0 tracked** — with chemistry's
**1,543 tracked** as the control proving those zeros are real and not an artifact of the query. `git archive`
therefore returns none of them, and git is not a backup route for this material.

A withdrawal that touched `01-source/` would convert a reversible publication decision into an irreversible
licence loss. Separating the two is the whole substance of this record.

### The published surface is regenerable; the source is not

`books/*/05-publication/` is build output, produced by `cnxml-render.js` from `03-translated/`, and
`static/content/<book>/` in the sister repo is gitignored build output regenerated by a sync. Both can be
rebuilt from material this decision preserves. That asymmetry is why the ruling can be aggressive about the
published surface and conservative about everything upstream of it.

One measured exception, and it is the only artifact in the withdrawn set that is **not** regenerable:
`books/edlisfraedi-2e/05-publication/mt-preview/slug-map.mt-preview.json`. Per `CLAUDE.md` § C9, slug-map
entries are recorded once, at the moment a prune happens; re-rendering a chapter that no longer has a
duplicate records nothing. It is data, not build output, and survives this ruling as data.

### Editorial capacity, not machine translation cost, is the binding constraint

The re-MT of the two kept books is priced in ISK and is affordable. The measurement that decided the shape
of this ruling is the human one: the two-book re-MT yields on the order of tens of thousands of segments
requiring editorial review, against a few hundred segments applied across the whole project's history to
date. Five books do not become good by being translated; they become good by being edited. Two books is
what the available editorial capacity can carry to a standard worth publishing.

This is the same argument as `docs/decisions/2026-07-06-re-mt-vs-editor-fixes-and-openstax-remerge.md`,
applied one level up: that record chose where editorial effort goes within a book, this one chooses how
many books it is spread across.

### Withdrawal is an efni-side operation, and this was measured rather than assumed

The natural reading — that removing books from the website is the sister repo's work — is wrong, and acting
on it would produce a withdrawal that silently reverts.

`../namsbokasafn-vefur/scripts/sync-content.js` derives `availableBooks` by reading **this** repository's
`books/` directory and keeping any book with at least one numbered chapter directory under
`05-publication/{mt-preview,faithful}/chapters/`. Its destination-deletion sweep is keyed on that list. Two
consequences follow, both measured against the code:

- a vefur-side deletion is undone by the next unscoped sync, because the book is still "available" here;
- a sync **scoped to the two kept books removes nothing**, because the delete is scoped inside the per-book
  loop and never visits a withdrawn book's destination directory.

**The [LEAD] resolved this by ruling that nothing is deleted and the retirement lives in vefur's sync/build.**
That closes the question this paragraph opened, and it inverts the paragraph's own conclusion in a way worth
stating plainly: because every book keeps its `05-publication/` tree, **every book stays in `availableBooks`
permanently**, so vefur's stale-directory sweep can never fire by itself. The retirement therefore cannot be
an emergent consequence of efni doing or not doing something — **it has to be an explicit, named exclusion in
vefur**. The measurement above is what makes that a requirement rather than a preference.

### Reader-facing URLs need a deliberate answer, and the default is the bad one

The reader site serves an any-path SPA fallback: every page path returns HTTP 200 with an identical small
shell. There is no `410` mechanism anywhere in the sister repo. Withdrawing content without a status-code
lever therefore converts live pages into **indexed soft-404s** — pages that return 200 forever and are not
deindexed. Removing the books from the site's catalogue while leaving the sitemap generator to enumerate the
content directory is worse still: the URLs stay in the sitemap and keep returning 200.

A book-slug nginx redirect precedent is already deployed and verified live — but **the reversibility of this
decision argues against reusing it.** `return 301` is a *permanent* signal; search engines consolidate the old
URL away, and resuming a title later means undoing a redirect that has already been honoured and cached. For a
pause, 404 (gone for now) or 503 with `Retry-After` are the honest signals, and a `robots.txt` `Disallow:` is
the opposite of a fix — it prevents recrawl, and therefore prevents the pages from ever being re-evaluated.
This record notes the constraint; the choice and its sequencing belong to the register.

## Consequences

- **Commits the project to two books at reader-facing quality**, and to spending the editorial capacity that
  makes that meaningful rather than spreading it across five.
- **Forecloses nothing about the withdrawn books' future.** Because source, MT output, TM, glossaries and
  provenance are preserved, re-publishing any of the three later is a render-and-sync operation, not a
  re-acquisition. Reversal costs rendering time; it does not cost licence access. **This is the property the
  ruling was shaped to protect, and it holds only for as long as `01-source/` is intact.**
- **Raises the value of the off-box source backup rather than lowering it.** Once a book is unpublished, its
  rendered HTML stops being even a partial second representation of it, so `books/<slug>/01-source/` becomes
  the only copy of that book's value. For biology and physics this is literal: those media trees exist on one
  disk and in no git history. **Withdrawal makes the backup more load-bearing, not less** — a conclusion that
  runs opposite to the intuition that de-scoped work matters less.
- **Voids a class of open work** — items that exist only to serve readers of a withdrawn book — and
  **narrows** another class from five books to two. Which items, and their status, belong to the register.
- **Creates work nothing previously owned**: the withdrawal operation itself, across both repositories and the
  server's nginx configuration. Tracked in the register.
- **Requires an explicit book-exclusion mechanism in vefur's sync/build**, because nothing efni does can
  produce the retirement on its own (see *Reasoning*). Tracked in the register.
- **Keeps every retired book one build away from returning.** Because no artifact is deleted and no registry is
  rewritten, resuming a title is re-including it in the sync — not a re-acquisition, a re-render, or a
  reconstruction. **This is the property the "delete nothing" clause buys, and it is worth more than the disk
  space it costs.**
- **Does not authorise deleting any preserved artifact.** Voiding the *work item* that consumed an artifact is
  not authorisation to delete the artifact. Embed mappings, slug maps, reference translations and provenance
  files record something ephemeral and cannot be re-derived.

## Alternatives considered

1. **Keep all five published, fix defects as they surface.** Rejected: it spreads editorial capacity so thin
   that no book reaches a standard worth publishing, and it keeps five books' worth of pipeline defects
   reader-visible. The register's own backlog is the evidence — defects in books nobody was editing were
   being found faster than they were being fixed.
2. **Narrow to chemistry alone.** Rejected: organic chemistry is already extracted, already carries a
   translated exercise track no other book here has, and is the only book whose `01-source` may legitimately
   be refreshed from upstream. Its marginal cost over chemistry-alone is small and its marginal value is a
   second complete textbook.
3. **Delete the retired books from the repository entirely.** Rejected on licence grounds — see *Question*.
   Two of the three hold CC BY 4.0 copies that cannot be re-obtained at any price, and their media exist
   nowhere else. This alternative is the one the ruling exists to rule out explicitly, because "remove from the
   site" and "remove from the project" are one careless step apart.
4. **Leave the published material up and simply stop maintaining it.** Rejected: unmaintained published
   material is a standing claim of quality the project would no longer be making good on, and the three books
   carry known reader-visible defects that would remain live indefinitely.
