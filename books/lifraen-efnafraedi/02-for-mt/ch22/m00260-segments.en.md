Alpha Bromination of Carboxylic Acids

<!-- SEG:m00260:para:para-00001 -->
The [[i:α]] bromination of carbonyl compounds by Br[[sub:2]] in acetic acid is limited to aldehydes and ketones because acids, esters, and amides don’t enolize to a sufficient extent. Carboxylic acids, however, can be [[i:α]] brominated by a mixture of Br[[sub:2]] and PBr[[sub:3]] in the [[i:Hell–Volhard–Zelinskii (HVZ) reaction.]]

<!-- SEG:m00260:alt:fig-00001-alt -->
Heptanoic acid reacts with bromine, phosphorus tribromide, to form 2-bromoheptanoic acid in 90 percent yield

<!-- SEG:m00260:para:para-00002 -->
The Hell–Volhard–Zelinskii reaction is a bit more complex than it looks and actually involves [[i:α]] substitution of an acid bromide enol rather than a carboxylic acid enol. The process begins by reaction of the carboxylic acid with PBr[[sub:3]] to form an acid bromide plus HBr (). The HBr then catalyzes enolization of the acid bromide, and the resultant enol reacts with Br[[sub:2]] in an [[i:α]]-substitution reaction to give an [[i:α]]-bromo acid bromide. Addition of water hydrolyzes the acid bromide in a nucleophilic acyl substitution reaction and yields the [[i:α]]-bromo carboxylic acid product.

<!-- SEG:m00260:alt:fig-00002-alt -->
Carboxylic acid reacts with phosphorus tribromide to form two interconvertable acid bromide intermediates, which react with bromine to yield an alpha-bromo acid bromide, which further reacts with water to form an alpha-bromocarboxylic acid.
