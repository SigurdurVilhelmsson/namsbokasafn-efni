Stereochemistry of Thermal Electrocyclic Reactions

<!-- SEG:m00343:para:para-00001 -->
How can we predict whether conrotatory or disrotatory motion will occur in a given case? According to frontier orbital theory, the stereochemistry of an electrocyclic reaction is determined by the symmetry of the polyene HOMO. The electrons in the HOMO are the highest-energy, most loosely held electrons and are therefore most easily moved during reaction. For thermal reactions, the ground-state electron configuration is used to identify the HOMO; for [[term:photochemical reactions|term-00001]], the excited-state electron configuration is used.

<!-- SEG:m00343:para:para-00002 -->
Let’s look again at the thermal ring-closure of conjugated trienes. According to [[docref:m00341#fig-00002]], the HOMO of a conjugated triene in its ground state has lobes of like sign on the same side of the molecule, a symmetry that predicts disrotatory ring-closure. This disrotatory cyclization is precisely what is observed in the thermal cyclization of 2,4,6-octatriene. The 2[[i:E]],4[[i:Z]],6[[i:E]] isomer yields cis product; the 2[[i:E]],4[[i:Z]],6[[i:Z]] isomer yields trans product ([[xref:fig-00001]]).

<!-- SEG:m00343:caption:fig-00001-caption -->

<!-- SEG:m00343:alt:fig-00001-alt -->
Two thermocyclizations. (2E,4Z,6E)-2,4,6-octatriene undergoes disrotatory movement with heat to form cis-5,6-dimethyl-1,3-cyclohexadiene. (2E,4Z,6Z)-2,4,6-octatriene undergoes disrotatory movement with heat to form trans-5,6-dimethyl-1,3-cyclohexadiene.

<!-- SEG:m00343:para:para-00003 -->
In the same way, the ground-state HOMO of conjugated dienes ([[docref:m00341#fig-00001]]) has a symmetry that predicts conrotatory ring-closure. In practice, however, the conjugated diene reaction can be observed only in the reverse direction (cyclobutene [[MATH:1]] diene) because of the position of the equilibrium. We therefore find that the 3,4-dimethylcyclobutene ring [[i:opens]] in a conrotatory fashion. [[i:cis]]-3,4-Dimethylcyclobutene yields (2[[i:E]],4[[i:Z]])-2,4-hexadiene, and [[i:trans]]-3,4-dimethylcyclobutene yields (2[[i:E]],4[[i:E]])-2,4-hexadiene by conrotatory opening ([[xref:fig-00002]]).

<!-- SEG:m00343:caption:fig-00002-caption -->
[[b:Thermal ring-openings of [[i:cis]]- and [[i:trans]]-dimethylcyclobutene occur by conrotatory paths.]]

<!-- SEG:m00343:alt:fig-00002-alt -->
Two thermal ring opening reactions by conrotatory paths. Cis-3,4-dimethylcyclobutene reacts with heat to form (2E,4Z)-2,4-hexadiene. Trans-3,4-dimethylcyclobutene reacts with heat to form (2E,4E)-2,4-hexadiene.

<!-- SEG:m00343:para:para-00004 -->
Note that a conjugated diene and a conjugated triene react with opposite stereochemistry. The diene opens and closes by a conrotatory path, whereas the triene opens and closes by a disrotatory path. This is due to the different symmetries of the diene and triene HOMOs.

<!-- SEG:m00343:alt:fig-00003-alt -->
Orbital diagrams of diene and triene HOMO. Ends of diene HOMO are opposite sign. Ends of triene HOMO are same sign.

<!-- SEG:m00343:para:para-00005 -->
It turns out that there is an alternating relationship between the number of electron pairs (double bonds) undergoing bond reorganization and the stereochemistry of ring-opening or -closure. Polyenes with an even number of electron pairs undergo thermal electrocyclic reactions in a conrotatory sense, whereas polyenes with an odd number of electron pairs undergo the same reactions in a disrotatory sense.
