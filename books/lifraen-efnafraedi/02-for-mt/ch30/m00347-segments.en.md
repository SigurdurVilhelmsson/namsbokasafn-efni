Sigmatropic Rearrangements

<!-- SEG:m00347:para:para-00001 -->
A [[term:sigmatropic rearrangement|term-00001]], the third general kind of pericyclic reaction, is a process in which a [[i:σ]]-bonded substituent atom or group migrates across a [[i:π]] electron system from one position to another. A [[i:σ]] bond is broken in the reactant, the [[i:π]] bonds move, and a new [[i:σ]] bond is formed in the product. The [[i:σ]]-bonded group can be either at the end or in the middle of the [[i:π]] system, as the following [1,5] and [3,3] rearrangements illustrate:

<!-- SEG:m00347:alt:fig-00001-alt -->
A sigma bond breaks in 1,3-diene to form 1,3-diene with formed sigma bond via [1,5]sigmatropic rearrangement. An allylic vinylic ether forms unsaturated ketone with formed sigma bond via [3,3]sigmatropic rearrangement.

<!-- SEG:m00347:para:para-00002 -->
The notations [1,5] and [3,3] describe the kind of rearrangement that is occurring. The numbers refer to the two groups connected by the [[i:σ]] bond in the reactant and designate the positions in those groups to which migration occurs. For example, in the [1,5] sigmatropic rearrangement of a 1,3-diene, the two groups connected by the [[i:σ]] bond are a hydrogen atom and a pentadienyl group. Migration occurs to position 1 of the H group (the only possibility) and to position 5 of the pentadienyl group. In the [3,3] rearrangement of an allylic vinylic ether, the two groups connected by the [[i:σ]] bond are an allylic group and the vinylic ether. Migration occurs to position 3 of the allylic group and also to position 3 of the vinylic ether.

<!-- SEG:m00347:para:para-00003 -->
Like electrocyclic reactions and cycloadditions, sigmatropic rearrangements are controlled by orbital symmetries. There are two possible modes of reaction: migration of a group across the same face of the [[i:π]] system is suprafacial, and migration of a group from one face of the [[i:π]] system to the other face is antarafacial ([[xref:fig-00002]]).

<!-- SEG:m00347:caption:fig-00002-caption -->
[[b:Suprafacial and antarafacial sigmatropic rearrangements.]]

<!-- SEG:m00347:alt:fig-00002-alt -->
Orbital diagrams of suprafacial and antarafacial sigmatropic rearrangement. Antarafacial migrates groups to opposite face and suprafacial migrates groups to same face such that new bonds are always between same-sign lobes.

<!-- SEG:m00347:para:para-00004 -->
Both suprafacial and antarafacial sigmatropic rearrangements are symmetry-allowed, but suprafacial rearrangements are often easier for geometric reasons. The rules for sigmatropic rearrangements are identical to those for cycloaddition reactions ([[xref:table-00001]]).
