Electrocyclic Reactions

<!-- SEG:m00342:para:para-00001 -->
The best way to understand how orbital symmetry affects pericyclic reactions is to look at some examples. Let’s look first at a group of polyene rearrangements called [[i:electrocyclic reactions]]. An [[term:electrocyclic reaction|term-00001]] is a pericyclic process that involves the cyclization of a conjugated acyclic polyene. One [[i:π]] bond is broken, the other [[i:π]] bonds change position, a new [[i:σ]] bond is formed, and a cyclic compound results. For example, a conjugated triene can be converted into a cyclohexadiene, and a conjugated diene can be converted into a cyclobutene.

<!-- SEG:m00342:alt:fig-00001-alt -->
Two chemical reactions occuring in the presence of heat. A conjugated triene forms 1,3-cyclohexadiene. A conjugated diene forms cyclobutene.

<!-- SEG:m00342:para:para-00002 -->
Pericyclic reactions are reversible, and the position of the equilibrium depends on the specific case. In general, the triene [[MATH:1]] cyclohexadiene equilibrium favors the cyclic product, whereas the diene [[MATH:2]] cyclobutene equilibrium favors the less strained open-chain product.

<!-- SEG:m00342:para:para-00003 -->
The most striking feature of electrocyclic reactions is their stereochemistry. For example, (2[[i:E]],4[[i:Z]],6[[i:E]])-2,4,6-octatriene yields only [[i:cis]]-5,6-dimethyl-1,3-cyclohexadiene when heated, and (2[[i:E]],4[[i:Z]],6[[i:Z]])-2,4,6-octatriene yields only [[i:trans]]-5,6-dimethyl-1,3-cyclohexadiene. Remarkably, however, the stereochemical results change completely when the reactions are carried out under what are called [[i:photochemical]], rather than [[i:thermal]], conditions. Irradiation of (2[[i:E]],4[[i:Z]],6[[i:E]])-2,4,6-octatriene with ultraviolet light (denoted hν) yields [[i:trans]]-5,6-dimethyl-1,3-cyclohexadiene ([[xref:fig-00002]]).

<!-- SEG:m00342:caption:fig-00002-caption -->
[[b:Electrocyclic interconversions of 2,4,6-octatriene isomers and 5,6-dimethyl-1,3-cyclohexadiene isomers.]]

<!-- SEG:m00342:alt:fig-00002-alt -->
(2E,4Z,6E)-2,4,6-octatriene reacts with heat to give cis-5,6-dimethyl-1,3-cyclohexadiene. (2E,4Z,6E)-2,4,6-octatriene reacts with light to give trans-5,6-dimethyl-1,3-cyclohexadiene. (2E,4Z,6Z)-2,4,6-octatriene reacts with heat to give trans-5,6-dimethyl-1,3-cyclohexadiene.

<!-- SEG:m00342:para:para-00004 -->
A similar result is obtained for the thermal electrocyclic ring-opening of 3,4-dimethylcyclobutene. The trans isomer yields only (2[[i:E]],4[[i:E]])-2,4-hexadiene when heated, and the cis isomer yields only (2[[i:E]],4[[i:Z]])-2,4-hexadiene. On UV irradiation, however, the results are opposite. Cyclization of the 2[[i:E]],4[[i:E]] isomer under photochemical conditions yields cis product ([[xref:fig-00003]]).

<!-- SEG:m00342:caption:fig-00003-caption -->
[[b:Electrocyclic interconversions of 2,4-hexadiene isomers and 3,4-dimethylcyclobutene isomers.]]

<!-- SEG:m00342:alt:fig-00003-alt -->
Cis-3,4-dimethyl-cyclobutene reacts with heat to form (2E,4Z)-2,4-hexadiene. Cis-3,4-dimethyl-cyclobutene reacts with light to form (2E,4E)-2,4-hexadiene. Trans-3,4-dimethylcyclobutene reacts with heat to form (2E,4E)-2,4-hexadiene.

<!-- SEG:m00342:para:para-00005 -->
To account for these results, we need to look at the two outermost lobes of the polyene MOs—the lobes that interact when cyclization occurs. There are two possibilities: lobes of like sign can be either on the same side of the molecule or on opposite sides.

<!-- SEG:m00342:alt:fig-00004-alt -->
Two orbital pictures in which one figure has like lobes on the same side. The other figure has like lobes on the opposite side. The figures are separated by or.

<!-- SEG:m00342:para:para-00006 -->
For a bond to form, the outermost [[i:π]] lobes must rotate so that favorable bonding interaction is achieved—a positive lobe with a positive lobe or a negative lobe with a negative lobe. If two lobes of like sign are on the same side of the molecule, the two orbitals must rotate in [[i:opposite]] directions—one clockwise and one counterclockwise. This kind of motion is referred to as [[term:disrotatory|term-00002]].

<!-- SEG:m00342:alt:fig-00005-alt -->
An orbital diagram with arrows to show disrotatory (clockwise and counterclockwise) movement of two p-orbitals with same-sign lobes pointed upward. This allows same-sign lobes to merge.

<!-- SEG:m00342:para:para-00007 -->
Conversely, if lobes of like sign are on opposite sides of the molecule, both orbitals must rotate in the [[i:same]] direction, either both clockwise or both counterclockwise. This kind of motion is called [[term:conrotatory|term-00003]].

<!-- SEG:m00342:alt:fig-00006-alt -->
An orbital diagram with arrows to show conrotatory (clockwise and clockwise) movement of two p-orbitals with opposite-sign lobes pointed upward. This allows same-sign lobes to merge.
