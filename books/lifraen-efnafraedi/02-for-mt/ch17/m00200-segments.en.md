Naming Alcohols and Phenols

<!-- SEG:m00200:para:para-00001 -->
Alcohols are classified as primary (1°), secondary (2°), or tertiary (3°), depending on the number of organic groups bonded to the hydroxyl-bearing carbon.

<!-- SEG:m00200:alt:fig-00001-alt -->
The structures of primary, secondary, and tertiary alcohol. Primary alcohol has one R group, secondary has two R groups and tertiary has three R groups linked to the carbon atom.

<!-- SEG:m00200:para:para-00002 -->
Simple alcohols are named in the IUPAC system as derivatives of the parent alkane, using the suffix -[[i:ol.]]

<!-- SEG:m00200:para:para-00003 -->
[[b:RULE 1]][[BR]]Select the longest carbon chain containing the hydroxyl group, and derive the parent name by replacing the -[[i:e]] ending of the corresponding alkane with -[[i:ol.]] The -[[i:e]] is deleted to prevent the occurrence of two adjacent vowels: propanol rather than propaneol, for example.

<!-- SEG:m00200:para:para-00004 -->
[[b:RULE 2]][[BR]]Number the alkane chain beginning at the end nearer the hydroxyl group.

<!-- SEG:m00200:para:para-00005 -->
[[b:RULE 3]][[BR]]Number the substituents according to their position on the chain, and write the name, listing the substituents in alphabetical order and identifying the position to which the –OH is bonded. Note that in naming [[i:cis]]-1,4-cyclohexanediol, the final [[i:-e]] of cyclohexane is not deleted because the next letter, [[i:d,]] is not a vowel; that is, cyclohexanediol rather than cyclohexandiol. Also, as with alkenes (), newer IUPAC naming recommendations place the locant immediately before the suffix rather than before the parent.

<!-- SEG:m00200:alt:fig-00002-alt -->
The structures of three alcohols. The old names include 2-methyl-2-pentanol, cis-1,4-cyclohexanediol, and 3-phenyl-2-butanol. The new names of these structures are 2-methylpentan-2-ol, cis-cyclohexane-1,4-diol, and 3-phenylbutan-2-ol.
