Conjugate Carbonyl Additions: The Michael Reaction

<!-- SEG:m00285:para:para-00001 -->
We saw in that certain nucleophiles, such as amines, react with [[i:α]],[[i:β]]-unsaturated aldehydes and ketones to give a conjugate addition product, rather than a direct addition product.

<!-- SEG:m00285:alt:fig-00001-alt -->
The reversible reaction shows a conjugated carbonyl compound reacting with a nucleophile to form an intermediate with a carbanion. Further reaction of intermediate yields a conjugate addition product.

<!-- SEG:m00285:para:para-00002 -->
Exactly the same kind of conjugate addition can occur when a nucleophilic enolate ion reacts with an [[i:α]],[[i:β]]-unsaturated carbonyl compound—a process known as the [[term:Michael reaction|term-00001]] after Arthur Michael at Tufts College and Harvard University.

<!-- SEG:m00285:para:para-00003 -->
The best Michael reactions are those that take place when a particularly stable enolate ion, such as that derived from a [[i:β]]-keto ester or other 1,3-dicarbonyl compound, adds to an unhindered [[i:α]],[[i:β]]-unsaturated ketone. For example, ethyl acetoacetate reacts with 3-buten-2-one in the presence of sodium ethoxide to yield the conjugate addition product.

<!-- SEG:m00285:alt:fig-00002-alt -->
The reaction of ethyl acetoacetate with 3-buten-2-one with sodium ethoxide in ethanol, then hydronium ion, forms a conjugate addition product with an extended carbon chain.

<!-- SEG:m00285:para:para-00004 -->
Michael reactions take place by addition of a nucleophilic enolate ion donor to the [[i:β]] carbon of an [[i:α]],[[i:β]]-unsaturated carbonyl acceptor, according to the mechanism shown in [[xref:fig-00003]].

<!-- SEG:m00285:caption:fig-00003-caption -->
[[b:Mechanism of the Michael reaction between a [[i:β]]-keto ester and an [[i:α]],[[i:β]]-unsaturated ketone]]. The reaction is a conjugate addition of an enolate ion to the unsaturated carbonyl compound.

<!-- SEG:m00285:alt:fig-00003-alt -->
An arrow-pushing mechanism of the Michael reaction of ethyl-3-oxobutanoate involving three steps. These are proton abstraction, formation of an enolate, and product formation.

<!-- SEG:m00285:para:para-00005 -->
The Michael reaction occurs with a variety of [[i:α]],[[i:β]]-unsaturated carbonyl compounds, not just conjugated ketones. Unsaturated aldehydes, esters, thioesters, nitriles, amides, and nitro compounds can all act as the electrophilic acceptor component in Michael reactions ([[xref:table-00001]]). Similarly, a variety of different donors can be used, including [[i:β]]-diketones, [[i:β]]-keto esters, malonic esters, [[i:β]]-keto nitriles, and nitro compounds.

<!-- SEG:m00285:example-title:exam-00001-title -->
Using the Michael Reaction

<!-- SEG:m00285:para:para-00006 -->
How might you obtain the following compound using a Michael reaction?

<!-- SEG:m00285:para-title:para-00007-title -->
Strategy

<!-- SEG:m00285:para:para-00007 -->
A Michael reaction involves the conjugate addition of a stable enolate ion donor to an [[i:α]],[[i:β]]-unsaturated carbonyl acceptor, yielding a 1,5-dicarbonyl product. Usually, the stable enolate ion is derived from a [[i:β]]-diketone, [[i:β]]-keto ester, malonic ester, or similar compound. The C–C bond formed in the conjugate addition step is the one between the [[i:α]] carbon of the acidic donor and the [[i:β]] carbon of the unsaturated acceptor.

<!-- SEG:m00285:para-title:para-00008-title -->
Solution

<!-- SEG:m00285:alt:fig-00004-alt -->
The structure shows a cyclohexanone ring connected to C O O E t and C H 2 C H 2 C O O E t groups at the first carbon.

<!-- SEG:m00285:alt:fig-00005-alt -->
The reaction between ethyl-2-oxocyclohexanecarboxylate with alpha, beta-unsaturated ester (three-carbon) in the presence of sodium ethoxide in ethanol gives ethyl-1-(3-ethoxy-3-oxopropyl)-2-oxocyclohexanecarboxylate (1,5-dicarbonyl compound) with a new carbon-carbon bond between cyclohexane and ester.
