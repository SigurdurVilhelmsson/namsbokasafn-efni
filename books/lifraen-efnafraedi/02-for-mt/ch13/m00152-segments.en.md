NMR is used to help identify the product of nearly every reaction run in the laboratory. For example, we said in that hydroboration–oxidation of alkenes occurs with non-Markovnikov regiochemistry to yield the less highly substituted alcohol. With the help of NMR, we can now prove this statement.

<!-- SEG:m00152:para:para-00002 -->
Does hydroboration–oxidation of methylenecyclohexane yield cyclohexylmethanol or 1-methylcyclohexanol?

<!-- SEG:m00152:alt:fig-00001-alt -->
Methylenecyclohexane reacts first with borane and tetrahydrofuran, then hydrogen peroxide and hydroxide to form cyclohexylmethanol or 1-methylcyclohexanol; question mark near structures indicates product is unknown, could be either.

<!-- SEG:m00152:para:para-00003 -->
The [[sup:1]]H NMR spectrum of the reaction product is shown in [[xref:fig-00002]]a. The spectrum shows a two-proton peak at 3.40 [[i:δ]], indicating that the product has a –CH[[sub:2]]– group bonded to an electronegative oxygen atom (–CH[[sub:2]]OH). Furthermore, the spectrum shows no large three-proton singlet absorption near 1 [[i:δ]], where we would expect the signal of a quaternary –CH[[sub:3]] group to appear. ([[xref:fig-00002]]b) gives the spectrum of 1-methylcyclohexanol, the alternative product.) Thus, it’s clear that cyclohexylmethanol is the reaction product.
