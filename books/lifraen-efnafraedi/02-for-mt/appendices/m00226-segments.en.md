Glossary

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-1 -->
[[b:Absolute configuration]] (): The exact three-dimensional structure of a chiral molecule. Absolute configurations are specified verbally by the Cahn–Ingold–Prelog [[i:R]],[[i:S]] convention.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-2 -->
[[b:Absorbance ([[i:A]])]] (): In optical spectroscopy, the logarithm of the intensity of the incident light divided by the intensity of the light transmitted through a sample; [[i:A]] = log [[i:I]][[sub:0]]/[[i:I]].

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-3 -->
[[b:Absorption spectrum]] (): A plot of wavelength of incident light versus amount of light absorbed. Organic molecules show absorption spectra in both the infrared and the ultraviolet regions of the electromagnetic spectrum.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-4 -->
[[b:Acetals, R[[sub:2]]C(OR′)[[sub:2]]]] (): A type of functional group consisting of two −OR groups bonded to the same carbon, R[[sub:2]]C(OR′)[[sub:2]]. Acetals are often used as protecting groups for ketones and aldehydes.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-5 -->
[[b:Acetoacetic ester synthesis]] (): The synthesis of a methyl ketone by alkylation of an alkyl halide with ethyl acetoacetate, followed by hydrolysis and decarboxylation.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-6 -->
[[b:Acetyl group]] (): The CH[[sub:3]]CO− group.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-7 -->
[[b:Acetylide anion]] (): The anion formed by removal of a proton from a terminal alkyne, [[MATH:1]].

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-8 -->
[[b:Achiral]] (): Having a lack of handedness. A molecule is achiral if it has a plane of symmetry and is thus superimposable on its mirror image.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-9 -->
[[b:Acid anhydrides]] ([[docref:Chapter 21 Introduction|m00238]]): A type of functional group with two acyl groups bonded to a common oxygen atom, RCO[[sub:2]]COR′.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-10 -->
[[b:Acid halides]] ([[docref:Chapter 21 Introduction|m00238]]): A type of functional group with an acyl group bonded to a halogen atom, RCOX.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-11 -->
[[b:Acidity constant, [[i:K]][[sub:a]]]] (): A measure of acid strength. For any acid HA, the acidity constant is given by the expression [[MATH:2]].

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-12 -->
[[b:Activating groups]] (): Electron-donating groups such as hydroxyl (−OH) or amino (−NH[[sub:2]]) that increase the reactivity of an aromatic ring toward electrophilic aromatic substitution.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-13 -->
[[b:Activation energy]] (): The difference in energy between ground state and transition state in a reaction. The amount of activation energy determines the rate at which the reaction proceeds. Most organic reactions have activation energies of 40–100 kJ/mol.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-14 -->
[[b:Active site]] (, ): The pocket in an enzyme where a substrate is bound and undergoes reaction.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-15 -->
[[b:Acyclic diene metathesis (ADMET)]] (): A method of polymer synthesis that uses the olefin metathesis reaction of an open-chain diene.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-16 -->
[[b:Acyl group]] (, ): A −COR group.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-17 -->
[[b:Acyl phosphates]] ([[docref:Chapter 21 Introduction|m00238]]): A type of functional group with an acyl group bonded to a phosphate, RCO[[sub:2]]PO[[sub:3]][[sup:2−]].

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-18 -->
[[b:Acylation]] (, ): The introduction of an acyl group, −COR, onto a molecule. For example, acylation of an alcohol yields an ester, acylation of an amine yields an amide, and acylation of an aromatic ring yields an alkyl aryl ketone.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-19 -->
[[b:Acylium ion]] (): A resonance-stabilized carbocation in which the positive charge is located at a carbonyl-group carbon, [[MATH:3]]. Acylium ions are intermediates in Friedel–Crafts acylation reactions.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-20 -->
[[b:Adams’ catalyst]] (): The PtO[[sub:2]] catalyst used for alkene hydrogenations.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-21 -->
[[b:1,2-Addition]] (, ): Addition of a reactant to the two ends of a double bond.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-22 -->
[[b:1,4-Addition]] (, ): Addition of a reactant to the ends of a conjugated [[i:π]] system. Conjugated dienes yield 1,4 adducts when treated with electrophiles such as HCl. Conjugated enones yield 1,4 adducts when treated with nucleophiles such as amines.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-23 -->
[[b:Addition reactions]] (): Occur when two reactants add together to form a single product with no atoms left over.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-24 -->
[[b:Adrenocortical hormones]] (): Steroid hormones secreted by the adrenal glands. There are two types of these hormones: mineralocorticoids and glucocorticoids.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-25 -->
[[b:Alcohols]] (, [[docref:Chapter 17 Introduction|m00199]]): A class of compounds with an −OH group bonded to a saturated, [[i:sp]][[sup:3]]-hybridized carbon, ROH.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-26 -->
[[b:Aldaric acid]] (): The dicarboxylic acid resulting from oxidation of an aldose.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-27 -->
[[b:Aldehydes (RCHO)]] (, [[docref:Chapter 19 Introduction|m00211]]): A class of compounds containing the −CHO functional group.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-28 -->
[[b:Alditol]] (): The polyalcohol resulting from reduction of the carbonyl group of a sugar.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-29 -->
[[b:Aldol reaction]] (): The carbonyl condensation reaction of an aldehyde or ketone to give a [[i:β]]-hydroxy carbonyl compound.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-30 -->
[[b:Aldonic acids]] (): Monocarboxylic acids resulting from oxidation of the −CHO group of an aldose.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-31 -->
[[b:Aldoses]] (): A type of carbohydrate with an aldehyde functional group.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-32 -->
[[b:Alicyclic]] (): A nonaromatic cyclic hydrocarbon such as a cycloalkane or cycloalkene.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-33 -->
[[b:Aliphatic]] (): A nonaromatic hydrocarbon such as a simple alkane, alkene, or alkyne.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-34 -->
[[b:Alkaloids]] ([[docref:Chapter 2 [[i:Chemistry Matters]]|m00029#note-00001]]): Naturally occurring organic bases, such as morphine.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-35 -->
[[b:Alkanes]] (): A class of compounds of carbon and hydrogen that contains only single bonds.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-36 -->
[[b:Alkene]] (, [[docref:Chapter 7 Introduction|m00062]]): A hydrocarbon that contains a carbon–carbon double bond, [[MATH:4]].

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-37 -->
[[b:Alkoxide ion, RO[[sup:−]]]] (): The anion formed by deprotonation of an alcohol.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-38 -->
[[b:Alkoxymercuration]] (): A method for synthesizing ethers by mercuric-ion catalyzed addition of an alcohol to an alkene followed by demercuration on treatment with NaBH[[sub:4]].

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-39 -->
[[b:Alkyl group]] (): The partial structure that remains when a hydrogen atom is removed from an alkane.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-40 -->
[[b:Alkyl halide]] (, [[docref:Chapter 10 Introduction|m00112]]): A compound with a halogen atom bonded to a saturated, [[i:sp]][[sup:3]]-hybridized carbon atom.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-41 -->
[[b:Alkylamines]] (): Amino-substituted alkanes RNH[[sub:2]], R[[sub:2]]NH, or R[[sub:3]]N.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-42 -->
[[b:Alkylation]] (, , , ): Introduction of an alkyl group onto a molecule. For example, aromatic rings can be alkylated to yield arenes, and enolate anions can be alkylated to yield [[i:α]]-substituted carbonyl compounds.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-43 -->
[[b:Alkyne]] (, [[docref:Chapter 9 Introduction|m00102]]): A hydrocarbon that contains a carbon–carbon triple bond, [[MATH:5]].

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-44 -->
[[b:Allyl group]] (): A [[MATH:6]] substituent.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-45 -->
[[b:Allylic]] (): The position next to a double bond. For example, [[MATH:7]] is an allylic bromide.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-46 -->
[[b:[[i:α]]-Amino acids]] (): A type of difunctional compound with an amino group on the carbon atom next to a carboxyl group, RCH(NH[[sub:2]])CO[[sub:2]]H.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-47 -->
[[b:[[i:α]] Anomer]] (): The cyclic hemiacetal form of a sugar that has the hemiacetal −OH group cis to the −OH at the lowest chirality center in a Fischer projection.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-48 -->
[[b:[[i:α]] Helix]] (): The coiled secondary structure of a protein.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-49 -->
[[b:[[i:α]] Position]] ([[docref:Chapter 22 Introduction|m00256]]): The position next to a carbonyl group.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-50 -->
[[b:[[i:α]]-Substitution reaction]] ([[docref:Chapter 22 Introduction|m00256]]): The substitution of the [[i:α]] hydrogen atom of a carbonyl compound by reaction with an electrophile.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-51 -->
[[b:Amides]] (, [[docref:Chapter 21 Introduction|m00238]]): A class of compounds containing the −CONR[[sub:2]] functional group.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-52 -->
[[b:Amidomalonate synthesis]] (): A method for preparing [[i:α]]-amino acids by alkylation of diethyl amidomalonate with an alkyl halide followed by deprotection and decarboxylation.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-53 -->
[[b:Amines]] (, [[docref:Chapter 24 Introduction|m00273]]): A class of compounds containing one or more organic substituents bonded to a nitrogen atom, RNH[[sub:2]], R[[sub:2]]NH, or R[[sub:3]]N.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-54 -->
[[b:Amino acid]] (): [[i:See]] [[b:[[i:α]]-Amino acid.]]

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-55 -->
[[b:Amino sugar]] (): A sugar with one of its −OH groups replaced by −NH[[sub:2]].

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-56 -->
[[b:Amphiprotic]] (): Capable of acting either as an acid or as a base. Amino acids are amphiprotic.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-57 -->
[[b:Amplitude]] (): The height of a wave measured from the midpoint to the maximum. The intensity of radiant energy is proportional to the square of the wave’s amplitude.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-58 -->
[[b:Anabolic steroids]] (): Synthetic androgens that mimic the tissue-building effects of natural testosterone.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-59 -->
[[b:Anabolism]] (): The group of metabolic pathways that build up larger molecules from smaller ones.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-60 -->
[[b:Androgen]] (): A male steroid sex hormone.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-61 -->
[[b:Angle strain]] (): The strain introduced into a molecule when a bond angle is deformed from its ideal value. Angle strain is particularly important in small-ring cycloalkanes, where it results from compression of bond angles to less than their ideal tetrahedral values.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-62 -->
[[b:Annulation]] (): The building of a new ring onto an existing molecule.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-63 -->
[[b:Anomeric center]] (): The hemiacetal carbon atom in the cyclic pyranose or furanose form of a sugar.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-64 -->
[[b:Anomers]] (): Cyclic stereoisomers of sugars that differ only in their configuration at the hemiacetal (anomeric) carbon.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-65 -->
[[b:Antarafacial]] (): A pericyclic reaction that takes place on opposite faces of the two ends of a [[i:π]] electron system.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-66 -->
[[b:Anti conformation]] (): The geometric arrangement around a carbon–carbon single bond in which the two largest substituents are 180° apart as viewed in a Newman projection.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-67 -->
[[b:Anti periplanar]] (): Describing the stereochemical relationship in which two bonds on adjacent carbons lie in the same plane at an angle of 180°.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-68 -->
[[b:Anti stereochemistry]] (): The opposite of syn. An anti addition reaction is one in which the two ends of the double bond are attacked from different sides. An anti elimination reaction is one in which the two groups leave from opposite sides of the molecule.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-69 -->
[[b:Antiaromatic]] (): Referring to a planar, conjugated molecule with 4[[i:n π]] electrons. Delocalization of the [[i:π]] electrons leads to an increase in energy.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-70 -->
[[b:Antibonding MO]] (): A molecular orbital that is higher in energy than the atomic orbitals from which it is formed.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-71 -->
[[b:Anticodon]] (): A sequence of three bases on tRNA that reads the codons on mRNA and brings the correct amino acids into position for protein synthesis.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-72 -->
[[b:Antisense strand]] (): The template, noncoding strand of double-helical DNA that does not contain the gene.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-73 -->
[[b:Arene]] (): An alkyl-substituted benzene.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-74 -->
[[b:Arenediazonium salt]] (): An aromatic compound [[MATH:8]]; used in the Sandmeyer reaction.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-75 -->
[[b:Aromaticity]] ([[docref:Chapter 15 Introduction|m00180]]): The special characteristics of cyclic conjugated molecules, including unusual stability and a tendency to undergo substitution reactions rather than addition reactions on treatment with electrophiles. Aromatic molecules are planar, cyclic, conjugated species with 4[[i:n]] + 2 [[i:π]] electrons.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-76 -->
[[b:Arylamines]] (): Amino-substituted aromatic compounds, ArNH[[sub:2]].

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-77 -->
[[b:Atactic]] (): A chain-growth polymer in which the stereochemistry of the substituents is oriented randomly along the backbone.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-78 -->
[[b:Atomic mass]] (): The weighted average mass of an element’s naturally occurring isotopes.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-79 -->
[[b:Atomic number, [[i:Z]]]] (): The number of protons in the nucleus of an atom.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-80 -->
[[b:ATZ Derivative]] (): An anilinothiazolinone, formed from an amino acid during Edman degradation of a peptide.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-81 -->
[[b:Aufbau principle]] (): The rules for determining the electron configuration of an atom.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-82 -->
[[b:Axial bonds]] (): Bonds or positions in chair cyclohexane that lie along the ring axis, perpendicular to the rough plane of the ring.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-83 -->
[[b:Azide synthesis]] (): A method for preparing amines by S[[sub:N]]2 reaction of an alkyl halide with azide ion, followed by reduction.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-84 -->
[[b:Azo compounds]] (): A class of compounds with the general structure [[MATH:9]].

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-85 -->
[[b:Backbone]] (): The continuous chain of atoms running the length of a protein or other polymer.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-86 -->
[[b:Base peak]] (): The most intense peak in a mass spectrum.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-87 -->
[[b:Basicity constant, [[i:K]][[sub:b]]]] (): A measure of base strength in water. For any base B, the basicity constant is given by the expression [[MATH:10]]

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-88 -->
[[b:Bent bonds]] (): The bonds in small rings such as cyclopropane that bend away from the internuclear line and overlap at a slight angle, rather than head-on. Bent bonds are highly strained and highly reactive.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-89 -->
[[b:Benzoyl]] (): The C[[sub:6]]H[[sub:5]]CO− group.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-90 -->
[[b:Benzyl]] (): The C[[sub:6]]H[[sub:5]]CH[[sub:2]]− group.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-91 -->
[[b:Benzylic]] (): The position next to an aromatic ring.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-92 -->
[[b:Benzyne]] (): An unstable compound having a triple bond in a benzene ring.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-93 -->
[[b:[[i:β]] Anomer]] (): The cyclic hemiacetal form of a sugar that has the hemiacetal −OH group trans to the −OH at the lowest chirality center in a Fischer projection.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-94 -->
[[b:[[i:β]] Diketone]] (): A 1,3-diketone.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-95 -->
[[b:[[i:β]]-Keto ester]] (): A 3-oxoester.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-96 -->
[[b:[[i:β]] Lactam]] ([[docref:Chapter 21 [[i:Chemistry Matters]]|m00248#sect-00003]]): A four-membered lactam, or cyclic amide. Penicillin and cephalosporin antibiotics contain [[i:β]]-lactam rings.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-97 -->
[[b:[[i:β]]-Oxidation pathway]] (): The metabolic pathway for degrading fatty acids.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-98 -->
[[b:[[i:β]]-Pleated sheet]] (): A type of secondary structure of a protein.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-99 -->
[[b:Betaine]] (): A neutral dipolar molecule with nonadjacent positive and negative charges. For example, the adduct of a Wittig reagent with a carbonyl compound is a betaine.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-100 -->
[[b:Bicycloalkane]] (): A cycloalkane that contains two rings.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-101 -->
[[b:Bimolecular reaction]] (): A reaction whose rate-limiting step occurs between two reactants.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-102 -->
[[b:Block copolymers]] (): Polymers in which different blocks of identical monomer units alternate with one another.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-103 -->
[[b:Boat cyclohexane]] (): A conformation of cyclohexane that bears a slight resemblance to a boat. Boat cyclohexane has no angle strain but has a large number of eclipsing interactions that make it less stable than chair cyclohexane.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-104 -->
[[b:Boc derivative]] (): A butyloxycarbonyl N-protected amino acid.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-105 -->
[[b:Bond angle]] (): The angle formed between two adjacent bonds.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-106 -->
[[b:Bond dissociation energy, [[i:D]]]] (): The amount of energy needed to break a bond and produce two radical fragments.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-107 -->
[[b:Bond length]] (): The equilibrium distance between the nuclei of two atoms that are bonded to each other.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-108 -->
[[b:Bond strength]] (): An alternative name for bond dissociation energy.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-109 -->
[[b:Bonding MO]] (): A molecular orbital that is lower in energy than the atomic orbitals from which it is formed.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-110 -->
[[b:Branched-chain alkanes]] (): Alkanes that contain a branching connection of carbons as opposed to straight-chain alkanes.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-111 -->
[[b:Bridgehead]] (): An atom that is shared by more than one ring in a polycyclic molecule.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-112 -->
[[b:Bromohydrin]] (): A 1,2-bromoalcohol; obtained by addition of HOBr to an alkene.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-113 -->
[[b:Bromonium ion]] (): A species with a divalent, positively charged bromine, R[[sub:2]]Br[[sup:+]].

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-114 -->
[[b:Brønsted–Lowry acid]] (): A substance that donates a hydrogen ion (proton; H[[sup:+]]) to a base.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-115 -->
[[b:Brønsted–Lowry base]] (): A substance that accepts H[[sup:+]] from an acid.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-116 -->
[[b:C-terminal amino acid]] (): The amino acid with a free −CO[[sub:2]]H group at the end of a protein chain.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-117 -->
[[b:Cahn–Ingold–Prelog sequence rules]] (, ): A series of rules for assigning relative rankings to substituent groups on a chirality center or a double-bond carbon atom.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-118 -->
[[b:Cannizzaro reaction]] (): The disproportionation reaction of an aldehyde on treatment with base to yield an alcohol and a carboxylic acid.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-119 -->
[[b:Carbanion]] (, ): A carbon anion, or substance that contains a trivalent, negatively charged carbon atom (R[[sub:3]]C[[b::]][[sup:−]]). Alkyl carbanions are [[i:sp]][[sup:3]]-hybridized and have eight electrons in the outer shell of the negatively charged carbon.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-120 -->
[[b:Carbene, R[[sub:2]]C]] (): A neutral substance that contains a divalent carbon atom having only six electrons in its outer shell (R[[sub:2]]C[[b::]]).

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-121 -->
[[b:Carbinolamine]] (): A molecule that contains the R[[sub:2]]C(OH)NH[[sub:2]] functional group. Carbinolamines are produced as intermediates during the nucleophilic addition of amines to carbonyl compounds.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-122 -->
[[b:Carbocation]] (, ): A carbon cation, or substance that contains a trivalent, positively charged carbon atom having six electrons in its outer shell (R[[sub:3]]C[[sup:+]]).

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-123 -->
[[b:Carbohydrates]] ([[docref:Chapter 25 Introduction|m00274]]): Polyhydroxy aldehydes or ketones. Carbohydrates can be either simple sugars, such as glucose, or complex sugars, such as cellulose.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-124 -->
[[b:Carbonyl condensation reactions]] (): A type of reaction that joins two carbonyl compounds together by a combination of [[i:α]]-substitution and nucleophilic addition reactions.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-125 -->
[[b:Carbonyl group]] (, [[docref:Preview of Carbonyl Chemistry|m00255#sect-00009]]): The [[MATH:11]] functional group.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-126 -->
[[b:Carboxyl group]] (): The −CO[[sub:2]]H functional group.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-127 -->
[[b:Carboxylation]] (): The addition of CO[[sub:2]] to a molecule.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-128 -->
[[b:Carboxylic acids, RCO[[sub:2]]H]] (, [[docref:Chapter 20 Introduction|m00229]]): Compounds containing the −CO[[sub:2]]H functional group.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-129 -->
[[b:Carboxylic acid derivative]] ([[docref:Chapter 21 Introduction|m00238]]): A compound in which an acyl group is bonded to an electronegative atom or substituent that can act as a leaving group in a substitution reaction. Esters, amides, and acid halides are examples.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-130 -->
[[b:Catabolism]] (): The group of metabolic pathways that break down larger molecules into smaller ones.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-131 -->
[[b:Catalyst]] (): A substance that increases the rate of a chemical transformation by providing an alternative mechanism but is not itself changed in the reaction.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-132 -->
[[b:Cation radical]] (): A reactive species, typically formed in a mass spectrometer by loss of an electron from a neutral molecule and having both a positive charge and an odd number of electrons.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-133 -->
[[b:Chain-growth polymers]] (, , ): Polymers whose bonds are produced by chain reaction mechanisms. Polyethylene and other alkene polymers are examples.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-134 -->
[[b:Chain reaction]] (): A reaction that, once initiated, sustains itself in an endlessly repeating cycle of propagation steps. The radical chlorination of alkanes is an example of a chain reaction that is initiated by irradiation with light and then continues in a series of propagation steps.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-135 -->
[[b:Chair conformation]] (): A three-dimensional conformation of cyclohexane that resembles the rough shape of a chair. The chair form of cyclohexane is the lowest-energy conformation of the molecule.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-136 -->
[[b:Chemical shift]] (): The position on the NMR chart where a nucleus absorbs. By convention, the chemical shift of tetramethylsilane (TMS) is set at zero, and all other absorptions usually occur downfield (to the left on the chart). Chemical shifts are expressed in delta units ([[i:δ]]), where 1 [[i:δ]] equals 1 ppm of the spectrometer operating frequency.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-137 -->
[[b:Chiral]] (): Having handedness. Chiral molecules are those that do not have a plane of symmetry and are therefore not superimposable on their mirror image. A chiral molecule thus exists in two forms, one right-handed and one left-handed. The most common cause of chirality in a molecule is the presence of a carbon atom that is bonded to four different substituents.

<!-- SEG:m00226:item:list-00001-item-138 -->
[[b:Chiral environment]] (): The chiral surroundings or conditions in which a molecule resides.
