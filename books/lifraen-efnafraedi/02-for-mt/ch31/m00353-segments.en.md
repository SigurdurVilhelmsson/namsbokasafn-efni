Copolymers

<!-- SEG:m00353:para:para-00001 -->
Up to this point we’ve discussed only [[term:homopolymers|term-00001]]—polymers that are made up of identical repeating units. In practice, however, [[i:copolymers]] are more important commercially. [[term:Copolymers|term-00002]] are obtained when two or more different monomers are allowed to polymerize together. For example, copolymerization of vinyl chloride with vinylidene chloride (1,1-dichloroethylene) in a 1 : 4 ratio leads to the polymer Saran.

<!-- SEG:m00353:alt:fig-00001-alt -->
Vinyl chloride reacts with vinylidene chloride to form the polymer named saran depicted in parentheses. m denotes the vinyl chloride unit and n denotes vinylidene chloride unit in the product.

<!-- SEG:m00353:para:para-00002 -->
Copolymerization of monomer mixtures often leads to materials with properties quite different from those of either corresponding homopolymer, giving the polymer chemist a vast flexibility for devising new materials. Several different types of copolymers can be defined, depending on the distribution of monomer units in the chain. If monomer A is copolymerized with monomer B, for instance, the resultant product might have a random distribution of the two units throughout the chain, or it might have an alternating distribution.

<!-- SEG:m00353:alt:fig-00002-alt -->
The structures of random copolymer and alternating copolymer. The random copolymer has monomer units A and B distributed randomly. An alternating copolymer has monomer units in an alternating manner.

<!-- SEG:m00353:para:para-00004 -->
The exact distribution of monomer units depends on the initial proportions of the two reactant monomers and their relative reactivities. In practice, neither perfectly random nor perfectly alternating copolymers are usually found. Most copolymers have many random imperfections.

<!-- SEG:m00353:para:para-00005 -->
Two other forms of copolymers that can be prepared under certain conditions are called [[i:block copolymers]] and [[i:graft copolymers]]. [[term:Block copolymers|term-00003]] are those in which different blocks of identical monomer units alternate with each other; [[term:graft copolymers|term-00004]] are those in which homopolymer branches of one monomer unit are “grafted” onto a homopolymer chain of another monomer unit.

<!-- SEG:m00353:alt:fig-00003-alt -->
Two copolymer structures. Block: many units of monomer A are followed by many units of monomer B. Graft: chains of Bs branch off a stem of As.

<!-- SEG:m00353:para:para-00006 -->
Block copolymers are prepared by initiating the polymerization of one monomer as if growing a homopolymer chain and then adding an excess of the second monomer to the still-active reaction mix. Graft copolymers are made by gamma irradiation of a completed homopolymer chain in the presence of the second monomer. The high-energy irradiation knocks hydrogen atoms off the homopolymer chain at random points, thus generating new radical sites that can initiate polymerization of the added monomer.
