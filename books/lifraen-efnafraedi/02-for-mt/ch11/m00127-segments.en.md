Biological Substitution Reactions

<!-- SEG:m00127:para:para-00001 -->
Both S[[sub:N]]1 and S[[sub:N]]2 reactions are common in biological chemistry, particularly in the pathways for biosynthesis of the many thousands of plant-derived substances called terpenoids, which we saw briefly in the [[docref:Chapter 8 [[i:Chemistry Matters]]|m00101#sect-00002]] and will discuss in [[b:Section 27.5]]. Unlike what typically happens in the laboratory, however, the substrate in a biological substitution reaction is usually an organodiphosphate rather than an alkyl halide. Thus, the leaving group is the diphosphate ion, abbreviated PP[[sub:i]], rather than a halide ion. In fact, it’s useful to think of the diphosphate group as the “biological equivalent” of a halide. The dissociation of an organodiphosphate in a biological reaction is typically assisted by complexation to a divalent metal cation such as Mg[[sup:2+]] to help neutralize charge and make the diphosphate a better leaving group.

<!-- SEG:m00127:alt:fig-00001-alt -->
A reaction in parentheses shows alkyl chloride dissociating to R plus and Cl minus. Separately, an organodiphosphate dissociates to form R plus and diphosphate ion.

<!-- SEG:m00127:para:para-00002 -->
As an example, two S[[sub:N]]1 reactions occur during the biosynthesis of geraniol, a fragrant alcohol found in roses and used in perfumery. Geraniol biosynthesis begins with dissociation of dimethylallyl diphosphate to give an allylic carbocation, which reacts with isopentenyl diphosphate ([[xref:fig-00002]]). From the viewpoint of isopentenyl diphosphate, the reaction is an electrophilic alkene addition, but from the viewpoint of dimethylallyl diphosphate, the process is an S[[sub:N]]1 reaction in which the carbocation intermediate reacts with a double bond as the nucleophile.

<!-- SEG:m00127:caption:fig-00002-caption -->
[[b:Biosynthesis of geraniol from dimethylallyl diphosphate.]] Two S[[sub:N]]1 reactions occur, both with diphosphate ion as the leaving group.

<!-- SEG:m00127:alt:fig-00002-alt -->
The figure shows the biosynthesis reaction of geraniol. Multiple steps involving two S N 1 reactions produces geraniol from dimethylallyl diphosphate, where the diphosphate ion acts as a leaving group.

<!-- SEG:m00127:para:para-00003 -->
Following this initial S[[sub:N]]1 reaction, loss of the pro-[[i:R]] hydrogen gives geranyl diphosphate, itself an allylic diphosphate that dissociates a second time. Reaction of the geranyl carbocation with water in a second S[[sub:N]]1 reaction, followed by loss of a proton, then yields geraniol.

<!-- SEG:m00127:para:para-00004 -->
As another example, S[[sub:N]]2 reactions are involved in almost all biological methylations, which transfer a –CH[[sub:3]] group from an electrophilic donor to a nucleophile. The donor is [[i:S]]-adenosylmethionine (abbreviated SAM), which contains a positively charged sulfur (a sulfonium ion, ), and the leaving group is the neutral [[i:S]]-adenosylhomocysteine molecule. In the biosynthesis of epinephrine (adrenaline) from norepinephrine, for instance, the nucleophilic nitrogen atom of norepinephrine attacks the electrophilic methyl carbon atom of [[i:S]]-adenosylmethionine in an S[[sub:N]]2 reaction, displacing [[i:S]]-adenosylhomocysteine ([[xref:fig-00003]]). In effect, [[i:S]]-adenosylmethionine is simply a biological equivalent of CH[[sub:3]]Cl.
