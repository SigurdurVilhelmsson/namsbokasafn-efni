Oxidative Cleavage of Alkynes

<!-- SEG:m00108:para:para-00001 -->
Alkynes, like alkenes, can be cleaved by reaction with powerful oxidizing agents such as ozone or KMnO[[sub:4]], although the reaction is of little value and it is mentioned only for completeness. A triple bond is generally less reactive than a double bond, and yields of cleavage products can be low. The products obtained from cleavage of an internal alkyne are carboxylic acids; from a terminal alkyne, CO[[sub:2]] is formed as one product.

The first reaction is an internal alkyne producing two carboxylic acids. Second reaction is a terminal alkyne producing carboxylic acid and carbon dioxide. Potassium permanganate or ozone is the reagent.
