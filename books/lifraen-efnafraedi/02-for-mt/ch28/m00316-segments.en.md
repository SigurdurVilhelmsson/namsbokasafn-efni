DNA Synthesis

<!-- SEG:m00316:para:para-00001 -->
The ongoing revolution in molecular biology has brought with it an increased demand for the efficient chemical synthesis of short DNA segments, called [[i:oligonucleotides,]] or simply [[i:oligos.]] The problems of DNA synthesis are similar to those of peptide synthesis () but are more difficult because of the complexity of the nucleotide monomers. Each nucleotide has multiple reactive sites that must be selectively protected and deprotected at specific times, and coupling of the four nucleotides must be carried out in the proper sequence. Automated DNA synthesizers are available, however, that allow the fast and reliable synthesis of DNA segments up to 200 nucleotides in length.

<!-- SEG:m00316:para:para-00002 -->
DNA synthesizers operate on a principle similar to that of the Merrifield solid-phase peptide synthesizer (). In essence, a protected nucleotide is covalently bonded to a solid support, and one nucleotide at a time is added to the growing chain by the use of a coupling reagent. After the final nucleotide has been added, all the protecting groups are removed and the synthetic DNA is cleaved from the solid support. Five steps are needed:

<!-- SEG:m00316:para:para-00003 -->
[[b:STEP 1]][[BR]]The first step in DNA synthesis is to attach a protected deoxynucleoside to a silica (SiO[[sub:2]]) support by an ester linkage to the 3′ –OH group of the deoxynucleoside. Both the 5′ –OH group on the sugar and free –NH[[sub:2]] groups on the heterocyclic bases must be protected. Adenine and cytosine bases are protected by benzoyl groups, guanine is protected by an isobutyryl group, and thymine requires no protection. The deoxyribose 5′ –OH is protected as its [[i:p]]-dimethoxytrityl (DMT) ether.

<!-- SEG:m00316:alt:fig-00001-alt -->
The first step of D N A synthesis. A deoxynucleoside and silica support are reacted to form a product where -O-Ph is replaced by the silica support.

<!-- SEG:m00316:alt:fig-00002-alt -->
The figure shows the structure of four bases named N-protected adenine, N-protected guanine, N-protected cytosine, and thymine. Wavy line denotes bond extension.

<!-- SEG:m00316:para:para-00004 -->
[[b:STEP 2]][[BR]]The second step is removal of the DMT protecting group by treatment with dichloroacetic acid in CH[[sub:2]]Cl[[sub:2]]. The reaction occurs by an S[[sub:N]]1 mechanism and proceeds rapidly because of the stability of the tertiary, benzylic dimethoxytrityl cation.

<!-- SEG:m00316:alt:fig-00003-alt -->
The reaction of nucleoside with dichloroacetic acid in dichloromethane removes the D M T protecting group from the hydroxyl of the nucleoside attached to the silica surface in the product.

<!-- SEG:m00316:para:para-00005 -->
[[b:STEP 3]][[BR]]The third step is the coupling of the polymer-bonded deoxynucleoside with a protected deoxynucleoside containing a [[i:phosphoramidite]] group [R[[sub:2]]NP(OR)[[sub:2]]] at its 3′ position. The coupling reaction takes place in the polar aprotic solvent acetonitrile, requires catalysis by the heterocyclic amine tetrazole, and yields a [[i:phosphite,]] P(OR)[[sub:3]], as product. Note that one of the phosphorus oxygen atoms is protected by a [[i:β]]-cyanoethyl group, [[MATH:1]]. The coupling step takes place with better than 99% yield.

<!-- SEG:m00316:alt:fig-00004-alt -->
The third step of D N A synthesis. The reaction of two nucleosides in presence of tetrazole forms a product where the phosphoramidite group is replaced by the phosphite group.

<!-- SEG:m00316:para:para-00006 -->
[[b:STEP 4]][[BR]]With the coupling accomplished, the phosphite product is oxidized to a phosphate by treatment with iodine in aqueous tetrahydrofuran in the presence of 2,6-dimethylpyridine. The cycle of ([[b:1]]) deprotection, ([[b:2]]) coupling, and ([[b:3]]) oxidation is then repeated until an oligonucleotide chain of the desired sequence has been constructed.

<!-- SEG:m00316:alt:fig-00005-alt -->
The fourth step of D N A synthesis. It involves the oxidation of the phosphite product to phosphate by reaction with iodine along with 2,6 dimethylpyridine in aqueous tetrahydrofuran.

<!-- SEG:m00316:para:para-00007 -->
[[b:STEP 5]][[BR]]The final step is removal of all protecting groups and cleavage of the ester bond holding the DNA to the silica. All these reactions are done at the same time by treatment with aqueous NH[[sub:3]]. Purification by electrophoresis then yields the synthetic DNA.

<!-- SEG:m00316:alt:fig-00006-alt -->
The last step of D N A synthesis. Two deoxynucleosides of a polynucleotide chain react with aqueous ammonia. The product has no protected groups.
