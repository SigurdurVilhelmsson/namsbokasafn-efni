Acids and Bases: The Brønsted–Lowry Definition

<!-- SEG:m00024:para:para-00001 -->
Perhaps the most important of all concepts related to electronegativity and polarity is that of [[i:acidity]] and [[i:basicity.]] We’ll soon see, in fact, that the acid–base behavior of organic molecules explains much of their chemistry. You may recall from a course in general chemistry that two definitions of acidity are frequently used: the [[i:Brønsted–Lowry definition]] and the [[i:Lewis definition.]] We’ll look at the Brønsted–Lowry definition in this and the following three sections and then discuss the Lewis definition in [[b:Section 2.11]].

<!-- SEG:m00024:para:para-00002 -->
A [[term:Brønsted–Lowry acid|term-00001]] is a substance that donates a hydrogen ion, H[[sup:+]], and a [[term:Brønsted–Lowry base|term-00002]] is a substance that accepts a hydrogen ion. (The name [[i:proton]] is often used as a synonym for H[[sup:+]] because loss of the valence electron from a neutral hydrogen atom leaves only the hydrogen nucleus—a proton.) When gaseous hydrogen chloride dissolves in water, for example, a polar HCl molecule acts as an acid and donates a proton, while a water molecule acts as a base and accepts the proton, yielding chloride anion (Cl[[sup:–]]) and hydronium cation (H[[sub:3]]O[[sup:+]]). This and other acid–base reactions are reversible, so we’ll write them with double, forward-and-backward arrows.

<!-- SEG:m00024:para:para-00003 -->
Chloride ion, the product that results when the acid HCl loses a proton, is called the [[term:conjugate base|term-00003]] of the acid, and hydronium ion, the product that results when the base H[[sub:2]]O gains a proton, is called the [[term:conjugate acid|term-00004]] of the base. Other common mineral acids such as H[[sub:2]]SO[[sub:4]] and HNO[[sub:3]] behave similarly, as do organic acids such as acetic acid, CH[[sub:3]]CO[[sub:2]]H.

Notice that water can act either as an acid or as a base, depending on the circumstances. In its reaction with HCl, water is a base that accepts a proton to give the hydronium ion, H[[sub:3]]O[[sup:+]]. In its reaction with ammonia (NH[[sub:3]]), however, water is an acid that donates a proton to give ammonium ion (NH[[sub:4]][[sup:+]]) and hydroxide ion, HO[[sup:–]].
