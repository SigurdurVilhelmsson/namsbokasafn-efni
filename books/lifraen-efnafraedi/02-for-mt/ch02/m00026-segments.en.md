Compilations of p[[i:K]][[sub:a]] values like those in [[docref:m00025#table-00001]] and are useful for predicting whether a given acid–base reaction will take place, because H[[sup:+]] will always go [[i:from]] the stronger acid [[i:to]] the stronger base. That is, an acid will donate a proton to the conjugate base of a weaker acid, and the conjugate base of a weaker acid will remove a proton from a stronger acid. Since water (p[[i:K]][[sub:a]] = 15.74) is a weaker acid than acetic acid (p[[i:K]][[sub:a]] = 4.76), for example, hydroxide ion holds a proton more tightly than acetate ion does. Hydroxide ion will therefore react to a large extent with acetic acid, CH[[sub:3]]CO[[sub:2]]H, to yield acetate ion and H[[sub:2]]O.

<!-- SEG:m00026:para:para-00002 -->
Another way to predict acid–base reactivity is to remember that the product conjugate acid in an acid–base reaction must be weaker and less reactive than the starting acid and the product conjugate base must be weaker and less reactive than the starting base. In the reaction of acetic acid with hydroxide ion, for example, the product conjugate acid (H[[sub:2]]O) is weaker than the starting acid (CH[[sub:3]]CO[[sub:2]]H), and the product conjugate base (CH[[sub:3]]CO[[sub:2]][[sup:–]]) is weaker than the starting base (OH[[sup:–]]).

<!-- SEG:m00026:example-title:exam-00001-title -->
Predicting Acid Strengths from p[[i:K]][[sub:a]] Values

<!-- SEG:m00026:para:para-00003 -->
Water has p[[i:K]][[sub:a]] = 15.74, and acetylene has p[[i:K]][[sub:a]] = 25. Which is the stronger acid? Does hydroxide ion react to a significant extent with acetylene?

<!-- SEG:m00026:para-title:para-00004-title -->
Strategy

<!-- SEG:m00026:para:para-00004 -->
In comparing two acids, the one with the lower p[[i:K]][[sub:a]] is stronger. Thus, water is a stronger acid than acetylene and gives up H[[sup:+]] more easily.

<!-- SEG:m00026:para-title:para-00005-title -->
Solution

<!-- SEG:m00026:para:para-00005 -->
Because water is a stronger acid and gives up H[[sup:+]] more easily than acetylene, the HO[[sup:–]] ion must have less affinity for H[[sup:+]] than the [[MATH:1]] ion. In other words, the anion of acetylene is a stronger base than hydroxide ion, and the reaction will not proceed significantly as written.

<!-- SEG:m00026:example-title:exam-00002-title -->
Calculating [[i:K]][[sub:a]] from p[[i:K]][[sub:a]]

<!-- SEG:m00026:para:para-00006 -->
According to the data in [[docref:m00025#table-00001]], acetic acid has p[[i:K]][[sub:a]] = 4.76. What is its [[i:K]][[sub:a]]?

<!-- SEG:m00026:para-title:para-00007-title -->
Strategy

<!-- SEG:m00026:para:para-00007 -->
Since p[[i:K]][[sub:a]] is the negative logarithm of [[i:K]][[sub:a]], it’s necessary to use a calculator with an ANTILOG or INV LOG function. Enter the value of the p[[i:K]][[sub:a]] (4.76), change the sign (–4.76), and then find the antilog (1.74 × 10[[sup:–5]]).

<!-- SEG:m00026:para-title:para-00008-title -->
Solution

<!-- SEG:m00026:para:para-00008 -->
[[i:K]][[sub:a]] = 1.74 × 10[[sup:–5]].
