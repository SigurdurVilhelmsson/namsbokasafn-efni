Drawing Resonance Forms

<!-- SEG:m00023:para:para-00001 -->
Look back at the resonance forms of the acetate ion and the acetone anion shown in the previous section. The pattern seen there is a common one that leads to a useful technique for drawing resonance forms. In general, any three-atom grouping with a [[i:p]] orbital on each atom has two resonance forms:

<!-- SEG:m00023:alt:fig-00001-alt -->
Two resonance forms in a three-atom grouping of X, Y, and Z with one double bond in the structure shown separated by a double-headed arrow.

<!-- SEG:m00023:para:para-00002 -->
The atoms X, Y, and Z in the general structure might be C, N, O, P, S, or others, and the asterisk (*) might mean that the [[i:p]] orbital on atom Z is vacant, that it contains a single electron, or that it contains a lone pair of electrons. The two resonance forms differ simply by an exchange in position of the multiple bond and the asterisk from one end of the three-atom grouping to the other.

<!-- SEG:m00023:para:para-00003 -->
By learning to recognize such three-atom groupings within larger structures, resonance forms can be systematically generated. Look, for instance, at the anion produced when H[[sup:+]] is removed from 2,4-pentanedione by reaction with a base. How many resonance structures does the resultant anion have?

<!-- SEG:m00023:alt:fig-00002-alt -->
The chemical reaction of 2,4 – pentanedione with a base.

<!-- SEG:m00023:para:para-00004 -->
The 2,4-pentanedione anion has a lone pair of electrons and a formal negative charge on the central carbon atom, next to a [[MATH:1]] bond on the left. The [[MATH:2]] grouping is a typical one for which two resonance structures can be drawn.

<!-- SEG:m00023:alt:fig-00003-alt -->
An arrow mechanism of resonance in a 2,4-pentanedione anion.

<!-- SEG:m00023:para:para-00005 -->
Just as there is a [[MATH:3]] bond to the left of the lone pair, there is a second [[MATH:4]] bond to the right. Thus, we can draw a total of three resonance structures for the 2,4-pentanedione anion.

<!-- SEG:m00023:alt:fig-00004-alt -->
Three resonance forms of 2,4-pentanedione, separated by two double-headed arrows.

<!-- SEG:m00023:example-title:exam-00001-title -->
Drawing Resonance Forms for an Anion

<!-- SEG:m00023:para:para-00006 -->
Draw three resonance structures for the carbonate ion, CO[[sub:3]][[sup:2–]].

<!-- SEG:m00023:para-title:para-00007-title -->
Strategy

<!-- SEG:m00023:para:para-00007 -->
Look for three-atom groupings that contain a multiple bond next to an atom with a [[i:p]] orbital. Then exchange the positions of the multiple bond and the electrons in the [[i:p]] orbital. In the carbonate ion, each singly bonded oxygen atom with three lone pairs and a negative charge is adjacent to the [[MATH:5]] double bond, giving the grouping [[MEDIA:1]].

<!-- SEG:m00023:alt:media-1-alt -->
Condensed structure of O with two nonbonding pairs double-bonded to C, which is single-bonded to another O with three nonbonding pairs and a negative charge.

<!-- SEG:m00023:para-title:para-00008-title -->
Solution

<!-- SEG:m00023:para:para-00008 -->
Exchanging the position of the double bond and an electron lone pair in each grouping generates three resonance structures.

<!-- SEG:m00023:alt:fig-00005-alt -->
The chemical structure of a carbonate ion.

<!-- SEG:m00023:alt:fig-00007-alt -->
Three resonance structures of the carbonate ion, featuring the lone pairs and charges on the oxygen atoms in each case and separated by two double-headed arrows.

Drawing Resonance Forms for a Radical

<!-- SEG:m00023:para:para-00009 -->
Draw three resonance forms for the pentadienyl radical, where a [[b:radical]] is a substance that contains a single, unpaired electron in one of its orbitals, denoted by a dot ([[b:·]]).

<!-- SEG:m00023:para-title:para-00010-title -->
Strategy

<!-- SEG:m00023:para:para-00010 -->
Find the three-atom groupings that contain a multiple bond next to an atom with a [[i:p]] orbital.

<!-- SEG:m00023:para-title:para-00011-title -->
Solution

<!-- SEG:m00023:para:para-00011 -->
The unpaired electron is on a carbon atom next to a [[MATH:6]] bond, giving a typical three-atom grouping that has two resonance forms.

<!-- SEG:m00023:para:para-00012 -->
In the second resonance form, the unpaired electron is next to another double bond, giving another three-atom grouping and leading to another resonance form.

<!-- SEG:m00023:para:para-00013 -->
Thus, the three resonance forms for the pentadienyl radical are:

<!-- SEG:m00023:alt:fig-00008-alt -->
The chemical structure of pentadienyl radical, featuring an unpaired electron on the right-most carbon atom.

<!-- SEG:m00023:alt:fig-00009-alt -->
Two resonance forms of pentadienyl radical, featuring an unpaired electron on the right-most carbon atom in one case and the central carbon atom in the next, separated by a double-headed arrow.

<!-- SEG:m00023:alt:fig-00010-alt -->
Two resonance forms of pentadienyl radical, featuring an unpaired electron on the central carbon atom in one case and the left-most carbon atom in the next, separated by a double-headed arrow.

<!-- SEG:m00023:alt:fig-00011-alt -->
All three resonance forms of pentadienyl radical, separated by double-headed arrows.
