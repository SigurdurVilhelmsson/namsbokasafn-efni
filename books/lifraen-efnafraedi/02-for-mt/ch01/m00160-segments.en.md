Atomic Structure: Electron Configurations

<!-- SEG:m00160:para:para-00001 -->
The lowest-energy arrangement, or [[term:ground-state electron configuration|term-00001]], of an atom is a list of the orbitals occupied by its electrons. We can predict this arrangement by following three rules.

<!-- SEG:m00160:para:para-00002 -->
[[b:RULE 1]][[BR]]The lowest-energy orbitals fill up first, [[MATH:1]], according to the following graphic, a statement called the [[term:[[i:Aufbau principle]]|term-00002]]. Note that the 4[[i:s]] orbital lies between the 3[[i:p]] and 3[[i:d]] orbitals in energy.

<!-- SEG:m00160:alt:fig-00001-alt -->
The orbital arrangement featuring orbitals in order 1s; 2s; 2p-3s; 3p-4s; 3d-4p-5s; 4d-5p-6s; 4f-5d-6p; 5f-6d; and 6f. Arrows denote the order in which orbitals are filled with electrons.

<!-- SEG:m00160:para:para-00003 -->
[[b:RULE 2]][[BR]]Electrons act in some ways as if they were spinning around an axis, somewhat as the earth spins. This spin can have two orientations, denoted as up ([[b:↑]]) and down ([[b:↓]]). Only two electrons can occupy an orbital, and they must have opposite spins, a statement called the [[term:[[i:Pauli exclusion principle]]|term-00003]].

<!-- SEG:m00160:para:para-00004 -->
[[b:RULE 3]][[BR]]If two or more empty orbitals of equal energy are available, one electron occupies each with spins parallel until all orbitals are half-full, a statement called [[term:[[i:Hund’s rule]]|term-00004]].

<!-- SEG:m00160:para:para-00005 -->
Some examples of how these rules apply are shown in [[xref:table-00001]]. Hydrogen, for instance, has only one electron, which must occupy the lowest-energy orbital. Thus, hydrogen has a 1[[i:s]] ground-state configuration. Carbon has six electrons and the ground-state configuration 1[[i:s]][[sup:2]]2[[i:s]][[sup:2]]2[[i:p[[sub:x]]]][[sup:1]]2[[i:p[[sub:y]]]][[sup:1]], and so forth. Note that a superscript is used to represent the number of electrons in a particular orbital.
