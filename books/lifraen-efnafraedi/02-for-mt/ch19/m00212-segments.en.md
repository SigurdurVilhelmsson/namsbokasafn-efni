Naming Aldehydes and Ketones

<!-- SEG:m00212:para:para-00001 -->
Aldehydes are named by replacing the terminal -[[i:e]] of the corresponding alkane name with -[[i:al.]] The parent chain must contain the –CHO group, and the –CHO carbon is numbered as carbon 1. Note in the following examples that the longest chain in 2-ethyl-4-methylpentanal is actually a hexane, but this chain does not include the –CHO group and thus is not the parent.

<!-- SEG:m00212:alt:fig-00001-alt -->
The structures of ethanal (also called acetaldehyde), propanal (also called propionaldehyde) and 2-ethyl-4-methylpentanal. The carbon atoms are numbered in 2-ethyl-4-methylpentanal.

<!-- SEG:m00212:para:para-00002 -->
For cyclic aldehydes in which the –CHO group is directly attached to a ring, the ring name followed by [[i:carbaldehyde]] is used.

<!-- SEG:m00212:alt:fig-00002-alt -->
The structures of cyclohexanecarbaldehyde and 2-naphthalenecarbaldehyde. The C H O groups are on C 1 and C 2 positions of cyclohexanecarbaldehyde and 2-naphthalenecarbaldehyde, respectively.

<!-- SEG:m00212:para:para-00004 -->
Ketones are named by replacing the terminal -[[i:e]] of the corresponding alkane name with -[[i:one.]] The parent chain is the longest one that includes the ketone group, and the numbering begins at the end nearer the carbonyl carbon. As with alkenes () and alcohols (), the locant is placed before the parent name using older rules but before the suffix with the newer IUPAC guidelines. For example:

<!-- SEG:m00212:alt:fig-00003-alt -->
The structures of 3-hexanone (new name: hexan-3-one), 4-hexen-2-one (new name: hex-4-en-2-one), and 2,4-hexanedione (new name: hexane-2,4-dione). The carbon atoms in each structure are numbered.

<!-- SEG:m00212:para:para-00005 -->
A few ketones are allowed by IUPAC to retain their common names.

<!-- SEG:m00212:alt:fig-00004-alt -->
The structures of acetone (carbonyl with two methyls), acetophenone (carbonyl with methyl and phenyl), and benzophenone (carbonyl with two phenyls).

<!-- SEG:m00212:para:para-00006 -->
When it’s necessary to refer to the [[MATH:3]] group as a substituent, the name [[term:acyl (a-sil) group|term-00001]] is used and the name ending -[[i:yl]] is attached. Thus, –COCH[[sub:3]] is an [[i:acetyl]] group, –CHO is a [[i:formyl]] group, –COAr is an [[i:aroyl]] group, and –COC[[sub:6]]H[[sub:5]] is a [[i:benzoyl]] group.

<!-- SEG:m00212:alt:fig-00005-alt -->
The structures of acyl (C O R), acetyl (C O C H 3), formyl (C H O), and benzoyl (C O Ph) groups.

<!-- SEG:m00212:para:para-00007 -->
If other functional groups are present and the double-bonded oxygen is considered a substituent on a parent chain, the prefix [[i:oxo]]- is used. For example:

<!-- SEG:m00212:alt:fig-00006-alt -->
The structure of 3-oxohexanal comprises a six-carbon chain. C 1 is a C H O group, and C 3 is double-bonded to O.
