Naming Amines

<!-- SEG:m00289:para:para-00001 -->
Amines can be either alkyl-substituted ([[term:alkylamines|term-00001]]) or aryl-substituted ([[term:arylamines|term-00002]]). Although much of the chemistry of the two classes is similar, there are also substantial differences. Amines are classified as [[term:primary (RNH[[sub:2]])|term-00003]], [[term:secondary (R[[sub:2]]NH)|term-00004]], or [[term:tertiary (R[[sub:3]]N)|term-00005]], depending on the number of organic substituents attached to nitrogen. Thus, methylamine (CH[[sub:3]]NH[[sub:2]]) is a primary amine, dimethylamine [(CH[[sub:3]])[[sub:2]]NH] is a secondary amine, and trimethylamine [(CH[[sub:3]])[[sub:3]]N] is a tertiary amine. Note that this usage of the terms [[i:primary, secondary,]] and [[i:tertiary]] differs from our previous usage. When we speak of a tertiary alcohol or alkyl halide, we refer to the degree of substitution at the alkyl carbon atom, but when we speak of a tertiary amine, we refer to the degree of substitution at the nitrogen atom.

<!-- SEG:m00289:alt:fig-00001-alt -->
Three structures show tertiary-butyl alcohol (a tertiary alcohol), trimethylamine (a tertiary amine) and tertiary butyl amine (a primary amine).

<!-- SEG:m00289:para:para-00002 -->
Compounds containing a nitrogen atom with four attached groups also exist, but the nitrogen atom must carry a formal positive charge. Such compounds are called [[term:quaternary ammonium salts|term-00006]].

<!-- SEG:m00289:alt:fig-00002-alt -->
The structure of a quaternary ammonium salt, which is a nitrogen positive linked to four R groups and an X negative ion.

<!-- SEG:m00289:para:para-00003 -->
Primary amines are named in the IUPAC system in several ways. For simple amines, the suffix -[[i:amine]] is added to the name of the alkyl substituent. You might also recall from the chapter on [[docref:Benzene and Aromaticity|m00180]] that the aromatic phenylamine, H[[sub:2]]N–C[[sub:6]]H[[sub:5]], has the common name [[i:aniline.]]

<!-- SEG:m00289:alt:fig-00003-alt -->
The structures of tertiary-butyl amine, cyclohexylamine, and aniline. Aniline is enclosed in square parentheses.

<!-- SEG:m00289:para:para-00004 -->
Alternatively, the suffix -[[i:amine]] can be used in place of the final -[[i:e]] in the name of the parent compound.

<!-- SEG:m00289:alt:fig-00004-alt -->
The structures of 4,4-dimethylcyclohexanamine and 1,4-butanediamine. 4,4-dimethylcyclohexanamine has cyclohexane ring with two methyl groups on C4 and amine on C1. 1,4-butanediamine is four-carbon chain with amines on C1 and C4.

<!-- SEG:m00289:para:para-00005 -->
Amines with more than one functional group are named by considering the −NH[[sub:2]] as an [[i:amino]] substituent on the parent molecule.

<!-- SEG:m00289:alt:fig-00005-alt -->
The structures of 2-aminobutanoic acid, 2,4-diaminobenzoic acid and 4-amino-2-butanone. The carbon atoms of 2-aminobutanoic acid and 4-amino-2-butanone are numbered explicitly.

<!-- SEG:m00289:para:para-00006 -->
Symmetrical secondary and tertiary amines are named by adding the prefix [[i:di]]- or [[i:tri]]- to the alkyl group.

<!-- SEG:m00289:alt:fig-00006-alt -->
The structures of diphenylamine and triethylamine. Diphenylamine has two benzene rings linked to an N H group. Triethylamine has a nitrogen atom linked to three ethyl groups.

<!-- SEG:m00289:para:para-00007 -->
Unsymmetrically substituted secondary and tertiary amines are referred to as [[i:N]]-substituted primary amines. The largest alkyl group takes the parent name, and the other alkyl groups are considered [[i:N]]-substituents on the parent ([[i:N]] because they’re attached to nitrogen).

<!-- SEG:m00289:alt:fig-00007-alt -->
N,N-dimethylpropylamine has a nitrogen linked to two methyl and a propyl group. N-ethyl-N-methylcyclohexylamine has cyclohexane linked to a nitrogen. This is connected to methyl and ethyl group.

<!-- SEG:m00289:para:para-00008 -->
[[term:Heterocyclic amines|term-00007]]—compounds in which the nitrogen atom occurs as part of a ring—are also common, and each different heterocyclic ring system has its own parent name. The heterocyclic nitrogen atom is always numbered as position 1.

<!-- SEG:m00289:alt:fig-00008-alt -->
The structures of pyridine, pyrrole, quinoline, imidazole, indole, pyrimidine, pyrrolidine, and piperidine. The compounds are numbered from the nitrogen atom.
