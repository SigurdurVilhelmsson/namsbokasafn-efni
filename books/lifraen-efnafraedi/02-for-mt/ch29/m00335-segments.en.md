Conversion of Pyruvate to Acetyl CoA

<!-- SEG:m00335:para:para-00001 -->
Pyruvate, produced by catabolism of glucose (and by degradation of several amino acids), can undergo several further transformations depending on the conditions and on the organism. In the absence of oxygen, pyruvate can either be reduced by NADH to yield lactate [CH[[sub:3]]CH(OH)CO[[sub:2]][[sup:–]]] or, in yeast, fermented to give ethanol. Under typical aerobic conditions in mammals, however, pyruvate is converted by a process called [[i:oxidative decarboxylation]] to give acetyl CoA plus CO[[sub:2]]. ([[i:Oxidative]] because the oxidation state of the carbonyl carbon rises from that of a ketone to that of a thioester.)

<!-- SEG:m00335:para:para-00002 -->
The conversion occurs through a multistep sequence of reactions catalyzed by a complex of enzymes and cofactors called the [[i:pyruvate dehydrogenase complex.]] The process occurs in three stages, each catalyzed by one of the enzymes in the complex, as outlined in [[xref:fig-00002]]. Acetyl CoA, the ultimate product, then acts as fuel for the final stage of catabolism, the citric acid cycle.

<!-- SEG:m00335:title:sect-00001-title -->
Step 1 of [[xref:fig-00002]]: Addition of Thiamin Diphosphate

<!-- SEG:m00335:title:sect-00002-title -->
MECHANISM

<!-- SEG:m00335:para:para-00003 -->
The conversion of pyruvate to acetyl CoA begins by reaction of pyruvate with thiamin diphosphate, a derivative of vitamin B[[sub:1]]. Formerly called thiamin [[i:pyro]]phosphate, thiamin diphosphate is usually abbreviated as TPP. The spelling [[i:thiamine]] is also correct and frequently used.

<!-- SEG:m00335:para:para-00004 -->
The key structural element in thiamin diphosphate is the thiazolium ring—a five-membered, unsaturated heterocycle containing a sulfur atom and a positively charged nitrogen atom. The thiazolium ring is weakly acidic, with a p[[i:K]][[sub:a]] of approximately 18 for the ring hydrogen between N and S. Bases can therefore deprotonate thiamin diphosphate, leading to formation of an ylide much like the phosphonium ylides used in Wittig reactions (). As in the Wittig reaction, the TPP ylide is a nucleophile and adds to the ketone carbonyl group of pyruvate to yield an alcohol addition product.

<!-- SEG:m00335:title:sect-00003-title -->
Step 2 of [[xref:fig-00002]]: Decarboxylation

<!-- SEG:m00335:para:para-00005 -->
The TPP addition product, which contains an iminium ion [[i:β]] to a carboxylate anion, undergoes decarboxylation in much the same way that a [[i:β]]-keto acid decarboxylates in the acetoacetic ester synthesis (). The [[MATH:1]] bond of the pyruvate addition product acts like the [[MATH:2]] bond of a [[i:β]]-keto acid to accept electrons as CO[[sub:2]] leaves, giving hydroxyethylthiamin diphosphate (HETPP).

<!-- SEG:m00335:alt:fig-00003-alt -->
Thiamin addition product undergoes decarboxylation reaction that leads to the formation of hydroxyethylthiamin diphosphate and carbon dioxide as the product.

<!-- SEG:m00335:title:sect-00004-title -->
Step 3 of [[xref:fig-00002]]: Reaction with Lipoamide

<!-- SEG:m00335:para:para-00006 -->
Hydroxyethylthiamin diphosphate is an enamine ([[MATH:3]]), which, like all enamines, is nucleophilic (). It therefore reacts with the enzyme-bound disulfide lipoamide by nucleophilic attack on a sulfur atom, displacing the second sulfur in an S[[sub:N]]2-like process.

<!-- SEG:m00335:alt:fig-00004-alt -->
Hydroxyethylthiamin diphosphate reacts with lipoamide to form a hemithioacetal product. The square bracket parentheses show the lipoamide structure in which lipoic acid and lysine are labeled.

<!-- SEG:m00335:title:sect-00005-title -->
Step 4 of [[xref:fig-00002]]: Elimination of Thiamin Diphosphate

<!-- SEG:m00335:para:para-00007 -->
The product of the HETPP reaction with lipoamide is a hemithioacetal, which eliminates thiamin diphosphate ylide. This elimination is the reverse of the ketone addition in [[b:step 1]] and generates acetyl dihydrolipoamide.

<!-- SEG:m00335:alt:fig-00005-alt -->
Base deprotonation of a hemithioacetal decomposes to form acetyl dihydrolipoamide and T P P ylide.

<!-- SEG:m00335:title:sect-00006-title -->
Step 5 of [[xref:fig-00002]]: Acyl Transfer

<!-- SEG:m00335:para:para-00008 -->
Acetyl dihydrolipoamide, a thioester, undergoes a nucleophilic acyl substitution reaction with coenzyme A to yield acetyl CoA plus dihydrolipoamide. The dihydrolipoamide is then oxidized back to lipoamide by FAD (), and the FADH[[sub:2]] that results is in turn oxidized back to FAD by NAD[[sup:+]], completing the catalytic cycle.

<!-- SEG:m00335:alt:fig-00006-alt -->
Acetyldihydrolipamide undergoes nucleophilic acyl substitution with coenzyme A to yield acetyl CoA and dihydrolipoamide. Dihydrolipoamide is then reduced by flavin adenine dinucleotide to a lipoamide.
