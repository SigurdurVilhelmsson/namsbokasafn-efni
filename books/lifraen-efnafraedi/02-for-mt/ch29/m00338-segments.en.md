Catabolism of Proteins: Deamination

<!-- SEG:m00338:para:para-00001 -->
The catabolism of proteins is much more complex than that of fats and carbohydrates because each of the 20 [[i:α]]-amino acids is degraded through its own unique pathway. The general idea, however, is that ([[b:1]]) the [[i:α]] amino group is first removed as ammonia by a [[term:deamination|term-00001]] process, ([[b:2]]) the ammonia is converted into urea, and ([[b:3]]) the remaining amino acid carbon skeleton (usually an [[i:α]]-keto acid) is converted into a compound that enters the citric acid cycle.

<!-- SEG:m00338:alt:fig-00001-alt -->
Alpha-amino acids are demaninated to  alpha-keto acids and ammonia. The alpha-keto acids enter the Citric Acid cycle and converted into pyruvate, oxaloacetate, alpha-ketoglutarate, succinyl CoA fumarate acetoacetate or acetyl CoA.

<!-- SEG:m00338:title:sect-00001-title -->
Transamination

<!-- SEG:m00338:title:sect-00002-title -->
Step 1 of [[xref:fig-00003]]: Transimination

<!-- SEG:m00338:para:para-00004 -->
The first step in trans[[i:amination]] is trans[[i:imination]]—the reaction of the PLP–enzyme imine with an [[i:α]]-amino acid to give a PLP–amino acid imine plus expelled enzyme as the leaving group. The reaction occurs by nucleophilic addition of the amino acid –NH[[sub:2]] group to the [[MATH:1]] bond of the PLP imine, much as an amine adds to the [[MATH:2]] bond of a ketone or aldehyde in a nucleophilic addition reaction (). The protonated diamine intermediate undergoes a proton transfer and expels the lysine amino group in the enzyme to complete the step.

<!-- SEG:m00338:alt:fig-00004-alt -->
An enzyme-bound PLP-amino acid imine reacts with an amino acid to provide a diamine intermediate which decomposes to a PlP-amino acid imine and the free amino enzyme.

<!-- SEG:m00338:title:sect-00003-title -->
Steps 2–4 of [[xref:fig-00003]]: Tautomerization and Hydrolysis

<!-- SEG:m00338:para:para-00005 -->
Following formation of the PLP–amino acid imine in [[b:step 1]], a tautomerization of the [[MATH:3]] bond occurs in [[b:step 2]]. The basic lysine residue in the enzyme that was expelled as a leaving group during transimination deprotonates the acidic [[i:α]] position of the amino acid, with the protonated pyridine ring of PLP acting as the electron acceptor. Reprotonation occurs on the carbon atom next to the ring, generating a tautomeric product that is the imine of an [[i:α]]-keto acid with pyridoxamine phosphate, abbreviated PMP.

<!-- SEG:m00338:para:para-00006 -->
Hydrolysis of this PMP–[[i:α]]-keto acid imine then completes the first part of the transamination reaction. This is the mechanistic reverse of imine formation and occurs by nucleophilic addition of water to the imine, followed by proton transfer and expulsion of PMP as leaving group.

<!-- SEG:m00338:alt:fig-00005-alt -->
PLP-amino acid imine reacts with an amino-bound enzyme to form an alpha-keto imine, which isomerizes to an imine tautomer, upon hydrolysis provided  pyridoxamine phosphate and an alpha-keto acid.

<!-- SEG:m00338:para:para-00002 -->
Deamination is usually accomplished by a [[term:transamination|term-00002]] reaction in which the –NH[[sub:2]] group of the amino acid is exchanged with the keto group of [[i:α]]-ketoglutarate, forming a new [[i:α]]-keto acid plus glutamate. The overall process occurs in two parts, is catalyzed by aminotransferases, and involves participation of the coenzyme pyridoxal phosphate, abbreviated PLP, a derivative of pyridoxine (vitamin B[[sub:6]]). The aminotransferases differ in their specificity for amino acids, but the mechanism remains the same.

<!-- SEG:m00338:alt:fig-00002-alt -->
Alpha-amino acid reacts with alpha-ketoglutarate in the presence of pyridoxal phosphate to form an alpha-keto acid, glutamate. The square parentheses below show pyridoxal phosphate and pyridoxine.

<!-- SEG:m00338:para:para-00003 -->
The mechanism of the first part of transamination is shown in [[xref:fig-00003]]. The process begins with reaction between the [[i:α]]-amino acid and pyridoxal phosphate, which is covalently bonded to the aminotransferase by an imine linkage between the side-chain –NH[[sub:2]] group of a lysine residue in the enzyme and the PLP aldehyde group. Deprotonation/reprotonation of the PLP–amino acid imine effects tautomerization of the imine [[MATH:4]] bond, and hydrolysis of the tautomerized imine gives an [[i:α]]-keto acid plus pyridoxamine phosphate (PMP).

<!-- SEG:m00338:alt:fig-00003-alt -->

<!-- SEG:m00338:title:sect-00004-title -->
Regeneration of PLP from PMP

<!-- SEG:m00338:para:para-00007 -->
With PLP plus the [[i:α]]-amino acid now converted into PMP plus an [[i:α]]-keto acid, PMP must be transformed back into PLP to complete the catalytic cycle. The conversion occurs by another transamination reaction, this one between PMP and an [[i:α]]-keto acid, usually [[i:α]]-ketoglutarate. The products are PLP plus glutamate, and the mechanism is the exact reverse of that shown in [[xref:fig-00003]]. That is, PMP and [[i:α]]-ketoglutarate give an imine; the PMP–[[i:α]]-ketoglutarate imine undergoes tautomerization of the [[MATH:5]] bond to give a PLP–glutamate imine; and the PLP–glutamate imine reacts with a lysine residue on the enzyme in a transimination process to yield PLP–enzyme imine plus glutamate.

<!-- SEG:m00338:alt:fig-00006-alt -->
Pyridoxamine phosphate reacts with alpha-ketoglutarate to form a pyridoxal phosphate bound enzyme imine, with the release of water and glutamate.
