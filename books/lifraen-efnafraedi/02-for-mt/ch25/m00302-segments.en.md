Configurations of the Aldoses

<!-- SEG:m00302:para:para-00001 -->
An aldotetrose is a four-carbon sugar with two chirality centers. Thus, there are 2[[sup:2]] = 4 possible stereoisomeric aldotetroses, or two [[sc:D]],[[sc:L]] pairs of enantiomers named erythrose and threose.

<!-- SEG:m00302:para:para-00002 -->
Aldopentoses have three chirality centers and a total of 2[[sup:3]] = 8 possible stereoisomers, or four [[sc:D]],[[sc:L]] pairs of enantiomers. These four pairs are called ribose, arabinose, xylose, and lyxose. All except lyxose occur widely in nature. [[sc:D]]-Ribose is an important constituent of RNA (ribonucleic acid), [[sc:L]]-arabinose is found in many plants, and [[sc:D]]-xylose is found in wood.

<!-- SEG:m00302:para:para-00003 -->
Aldohexoses have four chirality centers and a total of 2[[sup:4]] = 16 possible stereoisomers, or eight [[sc:D]],[[sc:L]] pairs of enantiomers. The names of the eight are allose, altrose, glucose, mannose, gulose, idose, galactose, and talose. Only [[sc:D]]-glucose, from starch and cellulose, and [[sc:D]]-galactose, from gums and fruit pectins, are widely distributed in nature. [[sc:D]]-mannose and [[sc:D]]-talose also occur naturally but in lesser abundance.

<!-- SEG:m00302:para:para-00004 -->
Fischer projections of the four-, five-, and six-carbon [[sc:D]] aldoses are shown in [[xref:fig-00001]]. Starting with [[sc:D]]-glyceraldehyde, we can imagine constructing the two [[sc:D]] aldotetroses by inserting a new chirality center just below the aldehyde carbon. Each of the two [[sc:D]] aldotetroses then leads to two [[sc:D]] aldopentoses (four total), and each of the four [[sc:D]] aldopentoses leads to two [[sc:D]] aldohexoses (eight total). In addition, each of the [[sc:D]] aldoses in [[xref:fig-00001]] has an [[sc:L]] enantiomer, which is not shown.

<!-- SEG:m00302:caption:fig-00001-caption -->
[[b:Configurations of [[sc:D]] aldoses.]] The structures are arranged from left to right so that the –OH groups on C2 alternate right/left (R/L) across each series. Similarly, the –OH groups at C3 alternate two right/two left (2R/2L), the –OH groups at C4 alternate 4R/4L, and the –OH groups at C5 are to the right in all eight (8R). Each [[sc:D]] aldose has a corresponding [[sc:L]] enantiomer that is not shown.

<!-- SEG:m00302:alt:fig-00001-alt -->
Fisher projections of  aldoses, D-glyceraldehyde, D-erythrose, D-threose, D-ribose, D-arabinose, D-erythrose, D-xylose, D-lyxose, D-allose, D-altrose, D-glucose, D-mannose, D-gulose, D-idose, D-galactose, ang D-talose.

<!-- SEG:m00302:para:para-00005 -->
Louis Fieser of Harvard University long ago suggested a simple procedure for remembering the names and structures of the eight [[sc:D]] aldohexoses:

<!-- SEG:m00302:para:para-00006 -->
[[b:[[span:STEP 1|red-text]]]][[BR]]Set up eight Fischer projections with the –CHO group on top and the –CH[[sub:2]]OH group at the bottom.

<!-- SEG:m00302:para:para-00007 -->
[[b:[[span:STEP 2|red-text]]]][[BR]]At C5, place all eight –OH groups to the right ([[sc:D]] series).

<!-- SEG:m00302:para:para-00008 -->
[[b:[[span:STEP 3|red-text]]]][[BR]]At C4, alternate four –OH groups to the right and four to the left.

<!-- SEG:m00302:para:para-00009 -->
[[b:[[span:STEP 4|red-text]]]][[BR]]At C3, alternate two –OH groups to the right, two to the left.

<!-- SEG:m00302:para:para-00010 -->
[[b:[[span:STEP 5|red-text]]]][[BR]]At C2, alternate –OH groups right, left, right, left.

<!-- SEG:m00302:para:para-00011 -->
[[b:[[span:STEP 6|red-text]]]][[BR]]Name the eight isomers using the mnemonic “[[b:All alt]]ruists [[b:gl]]adly [[b:ma]]ke [[b:gu]]m [[b:i]]n [[b:gal]]lon [[b:ta]]nks.”

<!-- SEG:m00302:para:para-00012 -->
The structures of the four [[sc:D]] aldopentoses can be generated in a similar way and named by the mnemonic suggested by a Cornell University undergraduate: “[[b:RIB]]s ARe eXtra Lean.”
