Configurations of the Aldoses

<!-- SEG:m00302:para:para-00001 -->
An aldotetrose is a four-carbon sugar with two chirality centers. Thus, there are 2[[sup:2]] = 4 possible stereoisomeric aldotetroses, or two D,L pairs of enantiomers named erythrose and threose.

<!-- SEG:m00302:para:para-00002 -->
Aldopentoses have three chirality centers and a total of 2[[sup:3]] = 8 possible stereoisomers, or four D,L pairs of enantiomers. These four pairs are called ribose, arabinose, xylose, and lyxose. All except lyxose occur widely in nature. D-Ribose is an important constituent of RNA (ribonucleic acid), L-arabinose is found in many plants, and D-xylose is found in wood.

<!-- SEG:m00302:para:para-00003 -->
Aldohexoses have four chirality centers and a total of 2[[sup:4]] = 16 possible stereoisomers, or eight D,L pairs of enantiomers. The names of the eight are allose, altrose, glucose, mannose, gulose, idose, galactose, and talose. Only D-glucose, from starch and cellulose, and D-galactose, from gums and fruit pectins, are widely distributed in nature. D-mannose and D-talose also occur naturally but in lesser abundance.

<!-- SEG:m00302:para:para-00004 -->
Fischer projections of the four-, five-, and six-carbon D aldoses are shown in [[xref:fig-00001]]. Starting with D-glyceraldehyde, we can imagine constructing the two D aldotetroses by inserting a new chirality center just below the aldehyde carbon. Each of the two D aldotetroses then leads to two D aldopentoses (four total), and each of the four D aldopentoses leads to two D aldohexoses (eight total). In addition, each of the D aldoses in [[xref:fig-00001]] has an L enantiomer, which is not shown.

<!-- SEG:m00302:caption:fig-00001-caption -->
[[b:Configurations of [[i:D]] aldoses.]] The structures are arranged from left to right so that the –OH groups on C2 alternate right/left (R/L) across each series. Similarly, the –OH groups at C3 alternate two right/two left (2R/2L), the –OH groups at C4 alternate 4R/4L, and the –OH groups at C5 are to the right in all eight (8R). Each D aldose has a corresponding L enantiomer that is not shown.

<!-- SEG:m00302:alt:fig-00001-alt -->
Fisher projections of  aldoses, D-glyceraldehyde, D-erythrose, D-threose, D-ribose, D-arabinose, D-erythrose, D-xylose, D-lyxose, D-allose, D-altrose, D-glucose, D-mannose, D-gulose, D-idose, D-galactose, ang D-talose.

<!-- SEG:m00302:para:para-00005 -->
Louis Fieser of Harvard University long ago suggested a simple procedure for remembering the names and structures of the eight D aldohexoses:

<!-- SEG:m00302:para:para-00006 -->
[[b:STEP 1]][[BR]]Set up eight Fischer projections with the –CHO group on top and the –CH[[sub:2]]OH group at the bottom.

<!-- SEG:m00302:para:para-00007 -->
[[b:STEP 2]][[BR]]At C5, place all eight –OH groups to the right (D series).

<!-- SEG:m00302:para:para-00008 -->
[[b:STEP 3]][[BR]]At C4, alternate four –OH groups to the right and four to the left.

<!-- SEG:m00302:para:para-00009 -->
[[b:STEP 4]][[BR]]At C3, alternate two –OH groups to the right, two to the left.

<!-- SEG:m00302:para:para-00010 -->
[[b:STEP 5]][[BR]]At C2, alternate –OH groups right, left, right, left.

<!-- SEG:m00302:para:para-00011 -->
[[b:STEP 6]][[BR]]Name the eight isomers using the mnemonic “[[b:All alt]]ruists [[b:gl]]adly [[b:ma]]ke [[b:gu]]m [[b:i]]n [[b:gal]]lon [[b:ta]]nks.”

<!-- SEG:m00302:para:para-00012 -->
The structures of the four D aldopentoses can be generated in a similar way and named by the mnemonic suggested by a Cornell University undergraduate: “[[b:RIB]]s ARe eXtra Lean.”

Drawing a Fischer Projection

<!-- SEG:m00302:para:para-00013 -->
Draw a Fischer projection of L-fructose.

<!-- SEG:m00302:para-title:para-00014-title -->
Strategy

<!-- SEG:m00302:para:para-00014 -->
Because L-fructose is the enantiomer of D-fructose, simply look at the structure of D-fructose and reverse the configuration at each chirality center.

<!-- SEG:m00302:para-title:para-00015-title -->
Solution

<!-- SEG:m00302:alt:fig-00002-alt -->
Fischer projections of enantiomeric  D-fructose and L-fructose, non-superimposable mirror image stereoisomers in which every O-H and H pair is switched

<!-- SEG:m00302:example-title:exam-00002-title -->
Drawing a Fischer Projection of a Molecular Model

<!-- SEG:m00302:para:para-00016 -->
Draw the following aldotetrose as a Fischer projection, and identify it as a D sugar or an L sugar:

<!-- SEG:m00302:para-title:para-00017-title -->
Strategy

<!-- SEG:m00302:para:para-00017 -->
The Fischer projection of a monosaccharide is drawn vertically, with the carbonyl group at or near the top and the –CH[[sub:2]]OH group at the bottom. The interior –H and –OH are drawn to the sides, pointing out of the page toward the viewer.

<!-- SEG:m00302:para-title:para-00018-title -->
Solution

<!-- SEG:m00302:alt:fig-00003-alt -->
The ball-and-stick model of aldotetrose which is an aldose with four carbon atoms. The gray, black, and red spheres represent hydrogen, carbon, and oxygen atoms, respectively.

<!-- SEG:m00302:alt:fig-00004-alt -->
The steps to draw the Fischer projection of D-erythrose form its ball-and-stick model. The model undergoes 90 degree-rotation so that the carbonyl group is at the top.
